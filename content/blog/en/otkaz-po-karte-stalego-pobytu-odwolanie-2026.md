# Permanent Residence Refusal in Poland: Challenging an Odmowa in 2026

Five years of collecting stamps, certificates and PIT returns, a wniosek (application) for karta stałego pobytu (permanent residence card) filed, twelve months of waiting - and then a decyzja odmowna (refusal decision) lands in your eDoręczenia. The first reaction is usually panic: it was all for nothing, the status is gone. In practice, in Mazowieckie roughly half of stały pobyt refusals rest on formal errors rather than a genuine lack of entitlement. And most of those can be challenged.

This is what you're up against in 2026: the grounds urzędy lean on most often, how the two-stage appeal works - odwołanie followed by skarga do WSA - which deadlines you cannot afford to miss, and what to actually attach to an appeal so it succeeds.

---

## What to do when an odmowa arrives

Receiving an odmowa does not strip your legal status overnight. You have 14 days from the date the decyzja was served to file an odwołanie (appeal) to the Szef Urzędu do Spraw Cudzoziemców (Head of the Office for Foreigners) through the wojewoda (regional governor) who issued the decision. While the appeal runs, the decyzja does not take effect, and the basis on which you were living in Poland before you applied stays valid.

This is the point people misread most. An odmowa on stały pobyt does not put you on a plane the next morning. Karta stałego pobytu is a bid for a new, better status. While you challenge it, the ground you held before you filed still applies: your temporary card, your visa, your UKR status. The break in status comes not from the refusal itself but from doing nothing - letting the 14 days run out.

So the first thing to do on the day the decyzja arrives is to record the date of service. Every deadline counts from that date. Then read the reasoning and work out exactly what you were refused for.

## The most common grounds for a PR refusal

The wojewoda has to justify an odmowa in writing, citing a specific ground and the relevant article of the law. A vague "you don't qualify" is not allowed. Here are the four reasons we see more than any others.

### An unnoticed break in the five years

The most frequent one. You're convinced you lived here five years without interruption, and then the urząd, checking stamps and dates, finds you went over the absence limit: more than 6 months total in a single year, or more than 10 months across the whole five-year period. Sometimes the break is a different kind - a gap of a few days between one card expiring and the next application, which resets the entire chain.

This is not always challengeable, but often it is. Sometimes the urząd miscounted the days, credited a trip that never happened, or ignored the fact that a timely application for the next card preserves continuity. We cover this in detail in the piece on [continuous five years and absences for PR](/en/blog/pmzh-nepreryvnost-5-let-vyezdy-2026).

### Insufficient income

There's no hard figure in the law; the wojewoda assesses whether your means are enough that you won't become a burden on the social system. The benchmark is the minimum wage (4 806 PLN gross in 2026) plus roughly 500 PLN per dependent. Income refusals often hit people whose earnings are seasonal, undocumented, or run through an umowa o dzieło (specific-task contract) with no regularity.

The key thing here: if the income genuinely exists but is documented badly, the refusal is beaten by supplying what was missing - PIT returns, contracts, bank statements.

### Mismatched addresses and dates

Meldunek (registered address) at one address, tenancy agreement at another, umowa o pracę (employment contract) showing a third. To an official that signals you don't actually live where you claim. These refusals sting the most, because the person really does live in Poland - they just never reconciled their paperwork to a single address.

### An unproven genuine marriage

For stały pobyt after three years of marriage to a Polish citizen, the wojewoda checks that the marriage is not a sham. If there's no shared registration, no joint account, no joint tenancy, and the spouses stumble over everyday details at the rozmowa (interview), an odmowa follows. It's challenged by assembling the proof of a shared life that wasn't attached the first time.

## How to read a refusal decision

A decyzja has three parts: the introduction (who applied and for what), the reasoning (uzasadnienie - why the refusal), and the operative part (the refusal itself and the appeal instructions - pouczenie). The part to read closely is the uzasadnienie: there the wojewoda has to name the facts and articles the conclusion is built on.

Our work starts by taking the uzasadnienie apart into individual claims and testing each one: does it match the case file, is the rule applied correctly, did the urząd ignore documents you actually submitted. Very often a decyzja carries a claim like "the applicant provided no proof of income" when the certificate was sitting in the file all along. That alone is a ready-made ground for an odwołanie.

The pouczenie always names the appeal body and the deadline. If the pouczenie is wrong (it happens), a missed deadline caused by a faulty pouczenie is a separate argument in your favour.

---

## Odwołanie: the first 14 days

The odwołanie is the first stage of appeal. It goes to the Szef Urzędu do Spraw Cudzoziemców in Warsaw, but physically you file it through the same wojewoda who issued the odmowa. So the document goes into the Mazowiecki Urząd Wojewódzki and the higher authority rules on it.

**The steps:**

1. **Record the date the decyzja was served.** The 14 days run on calendar days, from delivery in eDoręczenia or by post.
2. **Draft the odwołanie.** Formally, it's enough to state that you disagree with the decision - detailed argument is not required by the form. In reality, that argument decides the outcome. Say which of the wojewoda's findings are wrong, and why.
3. **Attach new evidence.** At this stage you can still supply the documents that were missing: PIT returns, certificates, proof of continuity, proof of cohabitation.
4. **File on time** - electronically through the same system you used for the wniosek, or on paper with a dated proof of dispatch. Even one day late closes this stage.
5. **Wait for the ruling.** The Szef Urzędu can uphold the odmowa, overturn it and issue the card, or send the case back to the wojewoda for reconsideration.

There is no fee for an odwołanie. While it is under review, the refusal decision is not enforced.

## Skarga do WSA: the next 30 days

If the Szef Urzędu upholds the refusal, the court stage remains - a skarga (complaint) to the Wojewódzki Sąd Administracyjny (Regional Administrative Court). The deadline is 30 days from service of the second-instance decision. Again, you file it through the body that issued the decision.

The WSA does not re-hear the case on the merits like a fresh wojewoda would - it reviews the legality of the decision: were the rules applied correctly, was the evidence gathered fully, was the procedure followed. So a skarga is built on legal arguments, not domestic ones. Here a lawyer is close to essential: the court isn't asking whether you're a good person, it's asking whether the urząd's decision holds up as a matter of law.

The court fee for a skarga is 200 PLN. If the WSA finds the decision unlawful, it annuls it and the case returns to the urząd for a fresh ruling that takes the court's position into account. Beyond that lies a cassation stage at the NSA (Naczelny Sąd Administracyjny), but few cases reach it.

## What's realistically challengeable, and what isn't

| Ground for refusal | Chance of overturning | What decides the outcome |
|--------------------|----------------------|--------------------------|
| Error in counting absence days | High | Accurate reconstruction of stamps and dates |
| Document was filed but not considered | High | Proof that it's in the file |
| Income exists but poorly documented | Medium-high | Supplying PIT, contracts, statements |
| Address mismatches | Medium | Reconciling documents to one address |
| Genuine marriage without proof | Medium | Gathering evidence of a shared life |
| A real break beyond the limit | Low | Sometimes changing the basis of the application |
| Sham marriage confirmed by inquiry | Very low | Almost never overturned |

The logic of the table is simple: you challenge an urząd error or a lack of proof, not the absence of the entitlement itself. If the five years genuinely broke because of a long trip, the fight is hard, and the honest move is to build a new strategy - for example, applying on a different basis.

---

## What to attach to the appeal

Bare disagreement convinces neither the court nor the Szef Urzędu. Evidence does the work:

- **Proof of continuity** - copies of every card, visa, decyzja and application receipt, closing off each day of the five years with no gaps.
- **Reconstruction of absences** - a table of trips with dates drawn from stamps and tickets, showing the limit wasn't exceeded.
- **Financial documents** - PIT returns for the relevant years, income certificates, statements, contracts.
- **A single address** - meldunek, tenancy agreement and bills all pointing to the same place of residence.
- **Proof of marriage** - joint tenancy, a shared account, photos, correspondence, witness statements.

Every document has to hit a specific claim in the uzasadnienie. Attaching "everything just in case" means drowning your strong arguments in noise.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many days do I have to appeal a PR refusal?**
You have 14 days from the date the decyzja was served to file an odwołanie to the Szef Urzędu do Spraw Cudzoziemców. If the second instance upholds the refusal, you then have 30 days for a skarga to the Wojewódzki Sąd Administracyjny. The deadlines run on calendar days, and missing one closes that stage.

**Will I be deported right after an odmowa on stały pobyt?**
No. An odmowa on stały pobyt does not cancel your prior basis of stay. While the appeal is running the decyzja is not enforced, and you remain legal on whatever status you held before you applied - a temporary card, a visa or UKR status.

**Do I have to pay for an odwołanie?**
There is no fee for an odwołanie to the Szef Urzędu. A skarga to the WSA carries a 200 PLN court fee. If the court annuls the decision as unlawful, costs may be recovered.

**Can I supply documents after the refusal?**
Yes. At the odwołanie stage you can still attach the missing evidence - PIT returns, certificates, proof of continuity, proof of cohabitation. At the WSA stage new evidence is barely accepted anymore; the court assesses the legality of the decision on the existing file.

**What if I missed the 14 days?**
There is a mechanism called przywrócenie terminu (restoration of the deadline), but it only works where the miss had a valid cause - illness, a faulty pouczenie, non-delivery. It's filed together with the odwołanie itself within 7 days of the cause ending. The odds depend on the circumstances, so it's far better not to let the deadline slip.

**Appeal alone or with a lawyer?**
A first-stage odwołanie you can file yourself if the urząd's error is obvious. But a skarga to the WSA is already a legal document about the legality of the decision, and a self-drafted one loses more often than not. Having the decyzja analysed before filing saves both time and the chance itself.
