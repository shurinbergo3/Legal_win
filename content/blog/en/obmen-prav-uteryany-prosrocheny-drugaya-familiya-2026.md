# Exchanging a Licence That Is Lost, Expired or in a Different Name

Exchanging a foreign licence for a Polish one is a clear enough procedure while the documents are in order. Real life hands you variations, though: the licence went missing in a move, it expired years ago, or your passport already shows your married name while the licence still carries your maiden one. In each case the standard script stalls and the urząd (office) asks for something you do not have to hand. Here is how to exchange a licence in these situations in 2026 without getting bogged down in correspondence with the wydział komunikacji (vehicle registration department).

---

## The general principle for tricky cases

When the data on the licence, the passport and the application diverge, or the document itself is missing, the wydział komunikacji cannot simply reissue the licence - it has to confirm that the licence really was issued, is valid, and belongs to you. So in non-standard cases extra documents come in: proof from the country of issue, a name-change certificate, sworn translations. The logic is always the same - close the gap with an official paper.

Once you see that, all three situations stop being scary: the question is not "can I exchange it" but "which document closes the gap".

---

## Case 1: the licence is lost or stolen

The most common headache. The card is physically gone, yet you need to exchange it for a Polish one. The key point: the urząd exchanges on the basis of data about your valid licence, so you have to confirm that it was issued and is current.

What is usually required:

- **A confirmation (proof of authenticity) from the authority that issued the licence** in your country of origin - stating that the licence was issued, for which categories, and whether it is valid;
- that confirmation is often requested through the issuing country's authority directly or through its consulate;
- in the event of theft, a police report is worth having, as it evidences how the licence was lost.

In practice, getting the confirmation from the country of issue is the longest stage. We help word and route the request properly, so the answer comes back in a form the Polish urząd will accept.

### And if the licence is lost in Poland after the exchange?

That is a different story - then it is about issuing a wtórnik (duplicate) of the Polish licence at the wydział komunikacji, and no foreign confirmation is needed. Do not confuse the two: exchanging a lost foreign licence and getting a duplicate of an already Polish one.

---

## Case 2: the licence has expired

Here it all turns on what "expired" means.

- If the **card's validity** has lapsed but the entitlement to drive still stands - many countries simply reissue the card, and confirmation from the country of issue will show the category is still yours. The exchange is then possible, but the officials may ask specifically for current proof of validity.
- If the **driving entitlement itself has expired** (not just the card), a one-for-one exchange may not be possible, and you might have to re-confirm your qualification. This is case-by-case and depends on the country and category.

Do not let it drag: the longer the document sits expired, the more questions the urząd has. And keep the [185-day rule](/en/blog/pravilo-185-dney-itd-shtrafy) in mind - a foreign licence loses effect six months after you settle in Poland, whether or not it is formally expired.

---

## Case 3: the licence is in a different name

A classic: the licence was issued in a maiden name, while the karta pobytu (residence card) and passport show the married name. To the urząd these are two different sets of data, and you have to prove it is one person.

What closes the gap:

1. **A marriage certificate** (or a name-change certificate) proving the move from one surname to the other;
2. if that document is foreign, it needs an **apostille** (or consular legalisation) and a **sworn translation** into Polish;
3. the chain of documents has to be unbroken: if the name changed twice, you need every link.

We often run exactly this bundle: [an apostille on foreign documents](/en/blog/apostille-inostrannyh-dokumentov-dlya-polshi-2026) plus a sworn translation of the marriage certificate, so the wydział komunikacji sees a logical progression of the surname and does not bounce the application.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I exchange a licence if the card is lost?**
Yes, but you will need confirmation from the issuing authority in your country that the licence was issued, is valid and for which categories. That request goes through the issuing country's authority or its consulate. In the event of theft, add a police report.

**How do I exchange an expired foreign licence?**
If only the card's validity has lapsed but the driving entitlement stands, the exchange is usually possible on current proof from the country of issue. If the driving entitlement itself has expired, a one-for-one exchange may not be possible and you may have to re-confirm your qualification - it depends on the country and category.

**What if the licence is in my maiden name but the passport shows a different one?**
You have to prove it is one person. For that you present a marriage certificate or a name-change certificate. If the document is foreign, it gets an apostille (or consular legalisation) and a sworn translation into Polish.

**Do I need a sworn translation for the exchange?**
Yes, the foreign licence and the accompanying documents (marriage certificate, confirmations) are translated by a tłumacz przysięgły (sworn translator) on the Polish register. The urząd will not accept an ordinary translation.

**How long does the exchange take in complex cases?**
Longer than the standard one, because the slowest stage is getting confirmation from the country of issue or legalising foreign documents. Plan with a margin and start requesting the missing papers as early as you can.
