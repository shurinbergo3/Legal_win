# The Passport Stamp (Stempel): What You Can and Cannot Do While Karta Pobytu Is Pending

You filed a wniosek (application) for a karta pobytu (residence card), a stamp went into your passport - and that is where clarity ends. Can you work now? Fly out for a funeral or to see family? Take a holiday and come back? More myths swirl around the stempel (stamp) in the passport than almost anything else, and the price of a mistake is high: one bad trip and you may not be able to return to Poland before your case is decided.

Below we sort out what this stamp actually gives you, what it does not, and how to keep a lawful wait for your card from turning into a status problem.

---

## What a Stempel Is and When It Goes In

The stempel (stamp) in your passport confirms that your wniosek for a karta czasowego pobytu (temporary residence card) was accepted in line with the formal requirements, and that your stay in Poland is lawful for the entire review period - even if your visa or visa-free window expired in the meantime. It is not a separate permit but a mark of lawful stay until the decyzja (decision) is issued.

The stamp is placed by the wojewoda (regional governor) after checking that your documents are complete. So time can pass between filing and the stamp - the urząd first looks at whether everything is in order.

### The Stamp and UPO Are Not the Same Thing

Many people confuse the stempel with the UPO. The difference is simple:

- **UPO** - an electronic confirmation that the application was received, arriving right after you file through the system. It records the fact of filing.
- **Stempel** - the mark in your passport, placed later, once the urząd has confirmed the documents were filed correctly and without formal defects.

In practice, what makes your stay lawful during the procedure is a correctly accepted application; the stempel simply makes that lawful stay visible in your passport. For plenty of everyday situations - renewing a lease, registering at a clinic, a conversation with an employer - having the physical stamp heads off needless questions.

---

## Does the Stamp Legalize Your Stay?

Yes. While a correctly filed wniosek is under review, your stay in Poland is lawful, and the stamp confirms it. You cannot be treated as illegal just because your visa or visa-free period ran out - provided the application was filed in time, before the previous basis expired.

The key word here is "in time." If you applied for a karta pobytu after your lawful stay had already ended, the stamp will not save the situation: the break in legal stay has already formed. That is exactly why you file well ahead, not on the last day.

---

## Does the Stamp Give You the Right to Work?

Here is where the most dangerous misconception begins. The stempel on its own does not grant the right to work. It legalizes your stay, not your employment.

Your right to work during the wait depends on your basis and on what you had before filing:

- if you applied **on the basis of work** and already held a valid zezwolenie na pracę (work permit) at the time of filing, or were working lawfully on the previous basis, you can usually work during the review;
- if you applied **on a different basis** (study, family) and had no separate right to work, the stamp does not create one;
- holders of certain statuses (for example, some categories with the right to work without a permit) keep that right - but the specific situation has to be checked.

Put simply, the stamp does not answer "can I work" - your basis does. That is the first thing to confirm, rather than finding out after the fact once the employer has already signed a contract.

---

## Can You Travel Abroad on the Stamp?

The most common and most costly question. The short answer: with a stamp in your passport there is no safe way to travel outside Poland.

The stempel confirms lawful stay **inside Poland**, but it is not a visa and gives no right of re-entry. If you leave Poland while the procedure is running, you will not be able to re-enter on the stamp. To return you need a valid visa or a visa-free basis - and if those have already expired, you are effectively locked out abroad, and the application may be treated as discontinued because you failed to appear for the required steps.

### And Inside Schengen?

There is a trap here too. The wojewoda's stamp is not a Schengen visa. Moving around Schengen on its strength is not legalized, and at the border or during a check that can turn into problems, up to an entry ban. Do not lean on the "Schengen is one zone" logic.

| Situation | With the stamp (no valid visa/visa-free basis) |
|-----------|------------------------------------------------|
| Staying and living in Poland | Lawful |
| Working | Only with grounds/a permit |
| Leaving Poland and returning | Cannot return |
| Trips around Schengen | Not legalized |

If travel really is necessary - serious family circumstances, for instance - it has to be planned ahead and, where possible, before filing, or you must hold a valid visa. In certain cases there are procedural solutions, but they have to be prepared, not improvised at the airport.

## Common Misconceptions About the Stamp

Over years of practice in Mazowieckie we see the same mistakes. Here they are in one list so you do not step on them:

1. **"Stamp = visa, I can fly off on holiday."** No. Leaving closes your way back until the case is decided.
2. **"I have a stamp, so I can work anywhere."** No. The right to work is set by your basis, not the stamp.
3. **"The stamp was not placed right away, so I must be illegal."** No. Lawful stay comes from a correctly accepted application; the stamp appears later.
4. **"With a stamp I can travel around Schengen."** No, it is not a Schengen visa.
5. **"The stamp lasts indefinitely."** It is valid for the review period; after the decision, your status is set by the decyzja and the card.

## What to Do While You Wait for the Decision

Waiting for a karta pobytu in Mazowieckie runs 8-14 months, sometimes longer. To get through that period smoothly:

- keep the stamped passport on you and make copies - it is asked for at the bank, by an employer, at the clinic;
- do not plan trips outside Poland without a valid visa or visa-free basis;
- if you need to travel, get advice before you buy the ticket;
- track your case so you do not miss a request for extra documents (uzupełnienie) - the deadline for it is limited;
- after a positive decision, remember [the 60-day rule for collecting the ready card](/en/blog/karta-pobytu-60-dney-na-otbor-2026).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What does the passport stamp give you when you apply for a karta pobytu?**
The stempel confirms that your stay in Poland is lawful for the entire review period of a correctly filed wniosek (application), even if your visa or visa-free window expired in the meantime. It is not a visa or a work permit, but a mark of lawful stay until the decyzja (decision) is issued.

**Can I work if there is a stamp in my passport?**
The stamp itself does not grant the right to work. Whether you can work depends on your basis: if you applied on the grounds of work and already held a zezwolenie na pracę (work permit) or worked lawfully before filing, you usually can. On other bases the stamp does not create the right to work.

**Can I leave Poland on the stamp and come back?**
No. The stamp legalizes your stay inside Poland but gives no right of re-entry. After leaving you cannot return on it - you would need a valid visa or a visa-free basis. Traveling during the procedure without one closes your way back.

**Is the wojewoda's stamp valid across Schengen?**
No. It is not a Schengen visa. Moving through other Schengen countries on the strength of the stamp is not legalized and can lead to problems at the border. For trips you need a valid visa or a visa-free basis.

**How is the stamp different from UPO?**
UPO is the electronic confirmation that the application was received, arriving right after filing. The stempel is the mark in your passport, placed later by the wojewoda once the documents are confirmed complete. Both relate to lawful stay during the procedure, but the stempel makes it visible in your passport.
