# Polish residence permit for remote work: what actually works in 2026

Start with the inconvenient part: **Poland has no dedicated digital nomad visa**. Unlike Spain, Portugal, Croatia or Hungary, Polish law contains no such category. An application built on "I work remotely for an American company and would like a residence permit" gets refused, because that basis does not exist in the Foreigners Act.

That does not close the route. It runs through a business basis, and it comes with specific financial thresholds.

## Why working for a foreign employer is not a basis

A work-based residence card (art. 114 of the Foreigners Act) presupposes a Polish employer and either a work permit or an exemption from one. A contract with a company in the US, the UK or the UAE meets none of that: no Polish employer, no work permit, no link to the Polish labour market.

The EU Blue Card works the same way - it requires an employer in Poland and a salary at 1.5 times the national average.

Which leaves one conclusion: **a remote worker has to legalise the source of income inside Poland, not merely arrange residence around a foreign contract**.

## Three routes that work

| Route | Legal basis | What you must prove |
|---|---|---|
| Sole trader (JDG) with B2B contracts abroad | art. 142 - economic activity | income above the threshold, or jobs created |
| Sp. z o.o. with you on the board | art. 142(3) | the same thresholds at company level |
| Studies in Poland plus freelancing | art. 144 - student permit | admission, paid tuition, means of support |

**Route 1: sole trader.** You register a jednoosobowa działalność gospodarcza, convert the foreign contract to B2B, pay Polish tax and contributions, and apply on business grounds. The fastest and most common path in IT, design, consulting and marketing.

An important limit: JDG is not open to every foreigner. Third-country nationals need a qualifying status - permanent residence card, EU long-term resident status, a temporary card issued on certain grounds, or UKR status. A national visa alone generally is not enough. We cover it in the article on [sole traders for foreigners](/en/blog/jdg-dlya-inostrancev-v-polshe).

**Route 2: Sp. z o.o.** You set up a company, join the board, and the company contracts with foreign clients. Open to any foreigner regardless of current status, and registrable remotely. Higher running costs, no status restrictions.

**Route 3: the student permit as a bridge.** Admission legalises the stay; during that period the business or company is set up, and the purpose of stay is changed afterwards - [how to do it without leaving Poland](/en/blog/zmiana-celu-pobytu-bez-vyezda-2026).

## The income thresholds

For a business-based residence card the wojewoda checks one of three conditions for the year preceding the application:

1. **Income** no lower than twelve times the average monthly gross salary in your voivodeship, per the GUS statistics for the previous year. In Mazowieckie that is on the order of PLN 100,000 a year, and the exact figure moves annually.
2. **Employment** of at least two people on indefinite full-time contracts - Polish citizens or foreigners entitled to work without a permit.
3. **Resources or activity** capable of meeting one of the first two conditions in future: a business plan, contracts, funds in an account, investment.

The third condition rescues those just starting, but the authority reads it strictly: signed contracts, bank statements and evidence of genuine activity, not a plan on a napkin.

Note the wording: **income, not turnover**. Optimising the tax bill to zero optimises away your residence basis at the same time. This is the classic mistake of running bookkeeping separately from immigration strategy.

## Tax forms and what they mean

| Form | Rate | Suits |
|---|---|---|
| Lump sum 12% | 12% of revenue | software development services |
| Lump sum 8.5% | 8.5% of revenue | consulting, marketing, some services |
| Flat tax | 19% of income | high costs, income above PLN 200,000 |
| Progressive scale | 12% / 32% | lower income, reliefs, children |

Plus the health contribution, whose rules shifted in 2026 - [covered separately](/en/blog/skladka-zdrowotna-2026-reforma).

The second tax question remote workers miss is **tax residency**. Spend more than 183 days a year in Poland, or move your centre of vital interests here, and you become a Polish tax resident with an obligation to declare worldwide income - [how that works](/en/blog/nalogovoye-rezidentstvo-polsha-183-dnya).

## A twelve-month plan

1. **Month 0.** Legal entry on a national visa or visa-free. Choose the structure: sole trader if your status allows, otherwise a company.
2. **Months 0-1.** Registration, tax and statistical numbers, bank account, social security. A company through S24 takes one to three days; a sole trader, one day.
3. **Month 1.** Address registration and PESEL. Without an address the wojewoda will ask questions; without a PESEL there is no trusted profile for MOS - [we complete zameldowanie in one to two days](/en/uslugi/meldunek).
4. **Months 1-2.** Move contracts to B2B, issue the first invoices and returns.
5. **Months 6-12.** Build the financial record: tax returns, statements, contracts. That record is your evidence.
6. **File through MOS v2.0.** Mazowieckie runs 8-14 months, and the UPO receipt legalises your stay throughout.

The core idea: a business-based residence card is not a document you collect on arrival. It is the product of a year of clean financial history, and that history starts in month one.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I live in Poland visa-free and work remotely for a foreign company?**
For 90 days in any 180, yes, provided you are not conducting activity in Poland in the legal sense. Long term, no: visa-free entry creates no basis for residence, and after 183 days you are a tax resident.

**Does employment by a foreign company without a Polish branch qualify?**
No. You need a Polish employer or your own Polish structure. A middle path is opening a branch or subsidiary, if your employer is willing.

**How much do I need to earn?**
As a 2026 benchmark in Mazowieckie, roughly PLN 100,000 of annual income. The precise figure comes from GUS statistics for the prior year and varies by voivodeship.

**What if there is no income in the first year?**
The third condition applies: evidence of resources and activity. That means contracts and funds, not statements of intent.

**Does time on a business card count towards permanent residence?**
Yes, in full - unlike time under temporary protection. Five years opens [EU long-term resident status](/en/blog/status-rezidenta-es-polsha-2026).

**Can I combine a sole trader business with an employment contract?**
Yes, and for the permit it helps: two income streams read as stability.
