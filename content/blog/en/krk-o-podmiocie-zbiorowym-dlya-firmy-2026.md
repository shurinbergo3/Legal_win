# Company criminal record certificate in Poland: when and how

Poland's National Criminal Register issues certificates not only for individuals but for companies as well. The document is the **zaświadczenie o niekaralności podmiotu zbiorowego** (certificate for a collective entity), confirming that the company does not appear in the register under the Act on the Liability of Collective Entities.

People usually discover it exists at an inconvenient moment: three days before a tender deadline, while applying for a transport licence, or when a foreign counterparty sends a document list containing "company criminal record certificate".

## When it is required

| Situation | What is asked for |
|---|---|
| Public procurement | certificate for the company plus every board member and commercial proxy |
| Licences: transport, security, finance, alcohol | certificate for the company and its managers |
| Tenders abroad | company certificate with apostille and sworn translation |
| Bank due diligence, larger financing | as the bank requires |
| Partner and vendor programmes | as the counterparty requires |
| Selling the company | a standard part of the pack |

Public procurement holds a trap: **the company certificate alone is not enough**. The contracting authority also checks individuals - board members, the commercial proxy, and in partnerships the partners or general partners. Where a foreigner sits on the board, a certificate from their country of citizenship joins the Polish one. [How to arrange that](/en/blog/spravka-o-nesudimosti-iz-strany-proishozhdeniya).

## How it differs from the personal certificate

Formally these are two different queries to the same register.

- **Query about a person** - for an individual, by PESEL or passport details.
- **Query about a collective entity** - for a legal person, by KRS and NIP number.

The forms are separate, and that is the first cause of rejected filings: the personal form gets submitted with company details, and the resulting document does not match the requirement.

The second difference: a collective entity certificate concerns the liability of **the company itself**, not the criminal records of its owners or directors. A clean company certificate says nothing about the CEO.

## Three ways to obtain it

**1. Online through e-KRK.** The Ministry of Justice system. It needs a qualified electronic signature or a Polish trusted profile held by a company representative. The fee is PLN 20 and the document arrives as a file, usually within a day.

**2. On paper.** At a KRK information point at a court, or by post to the KRK Information Bureau. The fee is PLN 30 and it takes anywhere from a few days to two weeks.

**3. Through a representative** under a power of attorney. The answer where directors hold no Polish trusted profile - typical for companies registered remotely by foreigners.

The electronic route is cheaper and faster, but carries the limitation below.

## The electronic certificate trap

An e-KRK certificate has full legal force **inside Poland**: contracting authorities, banks and public bodies accept it.

Abroad the problem starts. The MSZ issues apostilles only on paper originals bearing a signature and seal, and a printout of a file has neither. It then has to be notarised, or a paper version ordered from the start.

The rule is simple: **if the document leaves Poland, order paper**. We work through that logic in the article on [electronic documents and the apostille](/en/blog/e-apostille-elektronnye-dokumenty-2026).

## How long it stays valid

The statute sets no expiry; the receiving side sets the rule:

- **public procurement** - usually issued no earlier than six months before submission;
- **licences and concessions** - typically three months;
- **banks and foreign counterparties** - three months, occasionally six.

The practical conclusion: order against a specific deadline rather than "to have on file". At PLN 20-30, a repeat order costs less than a rejected bid.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does a sole trader need this certificate?**
No. A sole trader business is not a collective entity - the ordinary personal certificate for the entrepreneur applies.

**The company was registered a month ago. Will a certificate be issued?**
Yes; the register will simply show no entries. The company must be entered in the KRS and hold a NIP.

**Can an accountant or lawyer order it?**
Yes, under a power of attorney from the company. That is exactly how we work.

**What if there is an entry in the register?**
Then a legal review is needed: what the entry covers, when it lapses, and what steps are available.

**Does a certificate for the CEO replace the company one?**
No. Two different documents covering two different liability regimes; tenders normally require both.
