# Invitations to Poland (zaproszenie): who can invite and how it works

A zaproszenie is not a handwritten letter or a document printed at home. It is an **entry in the official register of invitations**, made by the wojewoda on the host's application. It costs PLN 27, takes up to a month, and confirms two things: the guest has somewhere to stay and someone to cover their costs.

The essential point first: **an invitation is not a visa and does not guarantee one**. The consul treats it as one document among several.

## Who is entitled to invite

The law limits the circle:

| Who | Condition |
|---|---|
| Polish citizen | residing in Poland |
| EU, EEA or Swiss national | residing in Poland with a right of residence |
| Foreigner | legal, uninterrupted residence in Poland for **at least 5 years**, or a permanent residence card, or EU long-term resident status |
| Legal person | with its seat in Poland |
| Other organisational unit | with its seat in Poland |

Hence the most common refusal: a foreigner holding a two-year temporary residence card **cannot** invite a relative. They need either five years of uninterrupted residence or an indefinite status - a [permanent residence card](/en/uslugi/karta-stalego-pobytu) or [EU long-term resident status](/en/uslugi/rezydent-ue).

A company may invite regardless of who owns it, which often makes it the faster route for business visits.

## What the host commits to

This is real financial liability, not a formality. The host undertakes to cover:

- the guest's **cost of stay**, including accommodation;
- the **cost of the return journey** to their country of origin or residence;
- **medical costs**, should they arise.

The obligation survives the guest's departure: if the state incurs costs for their removal or treatment, it may recover them from the host.

The wojewoda examines two conditions: **suitable accommodation** and **sufficient funds**. The financial threshold is calculated per invited person per month of declared stay, tied to social assistance income criteria and periodically updated - confirm the current figure before filing.

## The procedure step by step

1. **The host files the application** - in person at the voivodeship office, by post or electronically.
2. **Host documents**: identity document, title to the property (deed or lease), proof of income (tax return, salary certificate, bank statement).
3. **Guest details**: full passport data, purpose and length of visit, relationship or connection.
4. **Stamp duty of PLN 27** for the register entry.
5. **Processing** - up to 30 days, in practice two to four weeks depending on the voivodeship.
6. **Collection** - the host receives the original and passes it to the guest; the consul verifies the register entry.

The host states the validity period, which **cannot exceed one year**.

## When an invitation is needed and when it is not

**Useful or required:**

- for a type C visa for a private visit where the consul wants proof of purpose and funds;
- for a business visit on a Polish company's invitation;
- for family visits where the guest cannot evidence their own funds.

**Not required:**

- for EU nationals;
- for visa-free entry for 90 days, where the guest can show purpose and funds independently;
- for holders of a valid Schengen visa or residence card.

Separately: an invitation **grants no right to work**. A guest on a visit visa cannot work - that requires a work permit or a declaration, covered in our [article on the new employment rules](/en/blog/zezwolenie-na-prace-2026-praca-gov-pl).

## Common reasons for refusal

From our practice the wojewoda refuses registration for four reasons:

1. **The host does not qualify** - most often a foreigner without five years of residence.
2. **Insufficient funds** for the declared length of stay.
3. **No evidenced title to accommodation**, or floor space out of proportion to the number of guests.
4. **Doubts about the purpose** - inconsistencies in the data, or a history of refusals for the guest.

Refusals can be appealed to the Head of the Office for Foreigners within 14 days.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does an invitation guarantee a visa?**
No. The consul decides, weighing purpose, funds, ties to the country of residence and travel history.

**Can one application cover several people?**
Each invited person gets a separate entry and a separate fee, though the applications can be filed together.

**I hold a three-year residence card. Can I invite?**
No. Five years of uninterrupted legal residence or an indefinite status is required.

**How long is an invitation valid?**
Up to one year, as stated by the host. The visa itself is issued on the consul's terms.

**Can a company where I am a director invite?**
Yes, provided it is registered in Poland. That is the standard route for partners and contractors.

**What if the guest overstays?**
Costs connected with their stay and return fall on the host. Worth discussing with the guest before filing.
