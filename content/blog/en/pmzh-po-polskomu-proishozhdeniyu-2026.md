# Permanent Residence by Polish Descent: Stały Pobyt Without the Five-Year Wait

Most foreigners reach karta stałego pobytu (permanent residence card) the long way - five years of continuous residence, stamps, income certificates, the constant fear of a break in status. There's a shorter route. Permanent residence by Polish descent grants stały pobyt straight away, with no five-year record and no standard income requirement. You only have to prove that one of your ancestors was Polish, or hold a Karta Polaka (Pole's Card).

Here's who the law counts as a person of Polish descent, which documents prove it, why this is the fastest road to permanent status and onward to citizenship, and where people most often trip up - on archival records, the apostille and translations.

---

## Who can get stały pobyt by Polish descent

The right to karta stałego pobytu by descent applies if at least one parent, grandmother or grandfather was of Polish nationality - that is, back to one generation of ancestors (do jednego pokolenia). A separate, standalone basis is a valid Karta Polaka. In both cases you need neither five years of residence nor proof of stable income, as the general route demands.

Look closer. The law (Ustawa o cudzoziemcach, the Foreigners Act) grants stały pobyt to anyone who proves polskie pochodzenie (Polish descent) up to and including one generation of ancestors. In practice that means one of your parents or grandparents was Polish by nationality. A great-grandmother or great-grandfather formally falls outside the rule - but their documents are often needed as a link in the chain that proves the nationality of a closer ancestor.

The Karta Polaka is a short road of its own. If you hold a valid Karta Polaka, you can apply for stały pobyt directly, and there's no need to prove your ancestors' nationality again - the consul already confirmed it when the card was issued.

## Why this is the fastest route to PR

| Parameter | General route (5 years) | By Polish descent |
|-----------|-------------------------|-------------------|
| Residence record | 5 years continuous | Not required |
| Income requirement | Yes, stable | None as such |
| Continuity of absences | Strict limit | Not counted |
| Main evidence | Stamps, PIT, tenancy | Ancestor's descent |
| Card validity | 10 years | 10 years |

The difference is fundamental. On the general route you hold your status for years and pray a long trip won't reset the counter. By descent you can apply within your first months in the country - the question isn't how long you've lived here, it's who your ancestors were.

That's why we often tell clients with Polish roots not to take the well-worn path through czasowy pobyt (temporary residence) and five years of waiting, but to check the family tree first. If the roots hold up, you save years.

## Documents that prove descent

Assembling the evidence of descent is the most labour-intensive part. An ancestor's nationality isn't proven by a single sheet of paper but by a chain of documents, each tying one generation to the next.

**What usually works as proof:**

- **Civil status records** - birth, marriage and death certificates of ancestors that state nationality or establish the family link.
- **Archival documents** - metrical books, extracts from parish registers, pre-war records, papers from Polish and foreign archives.
- **Old passports and ID papers** - carrying the entry "Polak" or "narodowość polska".
- **Military, school and church records** - baptismal certificates, diplomas, service papers.
- **Karta Polaka** - if you have one, it replaces most of this chain.

The key word is chain. A grandmother's birth certificate on its own, with no link to you, proves little. You need an unbroken line: your birth certificate -> your parent's birth or marriage certificate -> your grandparent's document showing Polish nationality.

## Karta Polaka as a basis

If you hold a Karta Polaka, the road is noticeably shorter. Nationality has already been confirmed by the consul, and for stały pobyt you lean on the card itself rather than reassembling archives. It's the cleanest option of them all.

How to obtain the card in the first place, if you don't have it yet, we cover in a separate piece on [the Karta Polaka and how to get it](/en/blog/karta-polaka-kak-poluchit). The logic runs like this: sometimes it's faster to get a Karta Polaka first and apply for stały pobyt on its basis than to prove an ancestor's nationality to the wojewoda (regional governor) with a scatter of disparate archival papers.

But it's not a universal rule. If the family tree documents easily and the line is clean, you can go straight for PR by descent without a Karta Polaka at all.

## The link to citizenship

PR by descent isn't the finish line - it's a springboard to a Polish passport. Karta stałego pobytu is issued for 10 years with reissue, and after 3 years of living on stały pobyt the road to citizenship through naturalizacja (naturalisation) opens up.

For people with roots this is an especially logical route: the descent evidence you gather for stały pobyt is largely the same you'll need later. Archival records, a confirmed line of ancestors, legalised documents - all of it works for citizenship too. So assembling the family tree is an investment in two statuses at once.

Worth remembering about language: stały pobyt by descent doesn't require Polish, but citizenship does - you'll have to sit the egzamin z języka polskiego (Polish language exam) at B1 level. We go into this in the piece on [Polish B1 for citizenship](/en/blog/polski-b1-egzamin-do-obywatelstwa). It's better to start learning the language early rather than leaving it to the final year.

## Typical trouble with archives and legalisation

The problems almost always lie not in the law but in the documents. Ancestors lived a century ago, borders moved, archives burned, surnames were written now in Polish, now in Russian, now with a clerk's error.

### Documents scattered across archives

Records may sit in the archives of present-day Poland, Ukraine, Belarus or Lithuania - depending on where the ancestor was born before the borders were redrawn. Sometimes a targeted archival search is needed: a request to a specific state or church archive, work with a parish's metrical books.

### Discrepancies in surname spelling

Kowalczyk in one document, Kowalczuk in another, a Cyrillic rendering in a third. To the wojewoda these are different people until you prove they're one ancestor. Sometimes a separate confirmation that the spellings are identical is required.

### Apostille and sworn translations

Before you file them with the wojewoda, foreign civil status records need to be legalised - as a rule, an apostille (apostille) in the country of issue, followed by a tłumaczenie przysięgłe (sworn translation) into Polish. A missing apostille is a common reason the urząd returns the file. We cover the mechanics of legalisation in the article on [apostille, nostrification and sworn translations](/en/blog/apostille-nostryfikacja-tlumaczenia-przysiegle).

### Gaps in the chain

The hardest case is when a link goes missing between generations: no marriage certificate for a grandmother, a document destroyed, a record not preserved. The line is then rebuilt from indirect evidence - census returns, house registers, church records.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Who counts as a person of Polish descent?**
Anyone with at least one parent, grandmother or grandfather of Polish nationality - back to one generation of ancestors inclusive (do jednego pokolenia). Nationality is proven by the ancestor's documents: civil status records, archival entries, old passports marked with Polish narodowość. A valid Karta Polaka confirms descent on its own.

**Do I need five years of residence for stały pobyt by descent?**
No. That's the key difference from the general route. By Polish descent, or on the basis of a Karta Polaka, five years of continuous residence are not required, nor is proof of stable income. You can apply without having built up any record.

**Do I need Polish for PR by descent?**
For stały pobyt itself - no, the language isn't required. It comes into play later, for citizenship: there you sit the egzamin z języka polskiego at B1 level. So it's worth learning the language in advance if you're planning a passport.

**What if the archival documents haven't survived?**
The line of ancestors is rebuilt from indirect evidence - census entries, house registers, church (parish) books, documents of the ancestor's siblings. Sometimes a targeted request to a specific archive in Poland, Ukraine or Belarus is needed. A gap in one link doesn't always close the road.

**Is a Karta Polaka mandatory?**
No. A Karta Polaka is a convenient basis, because the consul has already confirmed nationality, but stały pobyt can also be obtained directly by descent, proving the ancestor's nationality with documents. If the line documents easily, a Karta Polaka isn't required.

**How quickly does descent lead to citizenship?**
Karta stałego pobytu grants permanent status straight away, and after 3 years of living on stały pobyt the road to citizenship through naturalizacja opens. The descent evidence gathered for PR is largely the same needed for the passport - you do the double work once.
