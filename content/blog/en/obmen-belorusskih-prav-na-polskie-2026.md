# Exchanging a Belarusian Driving Licence for a Polish One in 2026: Timelines and Nuances

Belarus is a party to the 1968 Vienna Convention on Road Traffic, so Belarusian driving licences are exchanged in Poland without theory or practical examinations for categories A and B. We have already covered this in our general guide to [exchanging Ukrainian and Belarusian licences](/en/blog/obmen-prav-na-polskie-2026). Here we go deeper into the Belarus-specific points - where clients most often hit delays: verification through the Belarusian MIA, the difference between the old and new licence formats, apostille, and how the consular channel works. Below - how to complete the procedure in Mazowieckie in 2026 and where not to lose unnecessary weeks.

## In Short: What You Need to Know in 30 Seconds

- **Exams:** for cat. A and B - not required (Vienna Convention). For C, D, C+E, D+E you need Kod 95 and badania
- **The key nuance:** the verification request goes to the Belarusian MIA via the Konsulat Białorusi w Warszawie (Belarusian consulate in Warsaw) or through the urząd - the reply is slower than for Ukraine
- **Waiting time:** usually 60-90 days
- **Licence format:** new plastic ID-1 cards exchange more smoothly; older formats carry a higher risk of an additional-confirmation request
- **Apostille:** may be needed for older licences or if the Belarusian MIA does not respond (~60 PLN + time)
- **Cost:** for cat. B usually 180-260 PLN (higher if apostille is needed)
- **Where:** Wydział Komunikacji (transport department) of the Urząd Dzielnicy in Warsaw according to your meldunek
- **Validity of the Polish licence:** 30 years for A and B (5 years for C, D)

## Who Must Exchange and When

Polish law (Ustawa o kierujących pojazdami, art. 14 - the Act on Drivers) requires that any person with an actual place of residence in Poland for **more than 185 days a year** hold a Polish prawo jazdy (driving licence). Foreign licences then lose their legal validity for driving - even if formally "recognised" under the convention.

There is no exception for Belarusians. Unlike Ukrainians with PESEL UKR, whose home licences remain valid until the end of temporary protection, Belarusian citizens must exchange their licence after 185 days of residence - with no transitional period for driving.

The 185 days are counted per calendar year. Proof includes the meldunek (address registration), a tenancy agreement, a ZUS entry, an employer's oświadczenie (statement). Driving with an invalidated licence is an offence: a fine of 200 to 2,000 PLN. More important than the fine is something else: in the event of an accident the insurer may refuse to pay out, leaving you to cover the entire loss yourself. More on penalties for delay - in the article on the [185-day rule and ITD fines](/en/blog/pravilo-185-dney-itd-shtrafy).

## Are Exams Required

No - for categories A and B no exams are taken. Belarus ratified the 1968 Vienna Convention on Road Traffic, and Poland accepts Belarusian licences for exchange directly, without theory or practice.

The exception is the professional categories. For C, D, C+E, D+E the exchange alone is not enough: you need **Kod 95** (the professional driver qualification) and valid badania - medical and psychological certificates. This is a separate process, which we cover in the [Kod 95 guide](/en/blog/kod-95-polsha-kak-poluchit). If you plan to work as a driver in Poland, it makes sense to run the licence exchange and Kod 95 in parallel - this saves 2-3 months.

If the licence is not Belarusian but issued by examination in a country outside the Vienna Convention (e.g. the USA, the UK, India), the procedure is different - there you cannot avoid the [WORD examination](/en/blog/zamena-prav-ssha-uk-india-word-ekzamen). For Belarusian licences, that is not your case.

## The Belarus-Specific Point: Verification Through the MIA and Apostille

This is the core point worth reading on for. The Polish urząd does not issue a licence "on trust" - it confirms the authenticity of your document through the issuing country. For Belarusian licences the request goes to the **Belarusian MIA**, and this channel is not as fast as the Ukrainian one.

The request is sent by one of two routes: either through the Konsulat Białorusi w Warszawie along the diplomatic line, or through the urząd itself via the CEPiK system. In practice the reply comes **more slowly than for Ukraine** - on average 60-90 days versus the Ukrainian 30-60. This is neither an error nor the fault of the urząd: that is how the inter-agency channel works.

What happens if the Belarusian MIA **does not respond** within a reasonable time, or your licence is of an **older format**? Then the urząd may require additional proof that the licence was issued. In practice this means:

- an **apostille** on the licence or on a certificate from Belarus confirming that the licence was indeed issued, or
- a **notarised copy + confirmation** of issuance.

An apostille costs around 60 PLN plus the time to obtain it. The downside is that the document has to be apostilled under Belarusian rules, which often requires contacting an authority in Belarus or going through the consular channel. So our advice: if you hold an older-format licence, do not wait for a request from the urząd - prepare the apostille **in advance**, in parallel with the translation. This protects you from a situation where the case "hangs" for 2-3 months waiting on the MIA's reply, and then for just as long while the apostille is being arranged.

## Old vs New Belarusian Licences

The main practical dividing line is the format of your document.

| Format | Description | Risk of an additional request | Apostille |
|---|---|---|---|
| **New (ID-1, plastic)** | an ID-1 standard card, with categories A, B, C, D etc. | low | usually not needed |
| **Old format** | documents of earlier generations, issued earlier | elevated | may be required |

**New plastic ID-1 licences** exchange noticeably more smoothly: the format is standardised, the data is easier to verify through the MIA channel, and the urząd less often requests additional documents.

**Older formats** carry a higher risk of a request for additional confirmation - it is precisely with these that the apostille question most often arises. If you are unsure which generation your document belongs to, assume the cautious scenario: prepare the apostille in advance.

## Where to Exchange in Warsaw

The wymiana białoruskiego prawa jazdy (exchange of a Belarusian driving licence) in Warsaw takes place at the Wydział Komunikacji of the Urząd Dzielnicy to which your meldunek belongs:

- Śródmieście - ul. Nowogrodzka 43
- Mokotów - ul. Rakowiecka 25/27
- Ursynów - al. Komisji Edukacji Narodowej 61
- Wola - al. Solidarności 90
- Praga-Południe - ul. Grochowska 274
- Bemowo - ul. Powstańców Śląskich 70

Booking - via the warszawa19115.pl portal or the Warszawa 19115 app. Section: "Wydział Komunikacji" → "Wymiana zagranicznego prawa jazdy". In the busy season, book 3 weeks ahead. If your meldunek is outside Warsaw, the exchange takes place at the Starostwo Powiatowe or the Wydział Komunikacji of the Urząd Miasta on the same principle - the meldunek decides which urząd you go to.

## Documents - Full List

The base package is the same for all categories. The differences concern additional documents for C, D, for old licences, and for cases involving a name change.

**Mandatory for everyone:**

- Wniosek o wydanie prawa jazdy - the application form completed in Polish (available at the Wydział Komunikacji counter or downloadable from warszawa19115.pl)
- The Belarusian licence - original + copy of both sides (clear photo, no glare or shadows)
- Tłumaczenie przysięgłe (sworn translation) of the licence into Polish by a tłumacz przysięgły of Belarusian - 80-110 PLN, done in 1-3 days
- Passport - original + copy of the photo page
- A document confirming legalny pobyt (legal residence): karta pobytu, a D visa, or meldunek + grounds
- Photo 35 × 45 mm - front-facing, no headwear, no older than 6 months
- Proof of payment: 100.00 PLN opłata komunikacyjna + 0.50 PLN opłata ewidencyjna

**Additionally for old licences (if the urząd requires it):**

- Apostille on the licence/certificate of issuance, or a notarised copy + confirmation of issuance (~60 PLN + time)

**Additionally for categories C, D, C+E, D+E:**

- Valid badania lekarskie (medical certificate) from a lekarz uprawniony - 180-250 PLN
- Valid badania psychologiczne (psychological certificate) - 150 PLN
- A Kod 95 certificate - for professional driving

**If you changed your surname:**

- A marriage or divorce certificate with a tłumaczenie przysięgłe
- The old passport with the former surname (if available)

## Step by Step

**Step 1. Translation (and apostille, if needed).** You go to a tłumacz przysięgły of Belarusian. You hand over a copy of the licence + a copy of the passport, and collect the translation in 1-3 days (80-110 PLN). If the licence is of an older format - **in advance**, without delay, you arrange the apostille or notarised confirmation. This is the most common source of delay, so we do it first.

**Step 2. Booking.** Through warszawa19115.pl you select the Wydział Komunikacji of your dzielnica → service "Wymiana zagranicznego prawa jazdy" → a free slot. In the busy season - 3 weeks ahead.

**Step 3. Visit to the urząd.** You bring all the documents. The urzędnik (official) accepts the wniosek, takes the original licence, issues a zaświadczenie (receipt confirmation), opens a PKK (Profil Kandydata na Kierowcę - the electronic case file) and sends a request to the Belarusian MIA via the CEPiK system or the consular channel.

**Step 4. Waiting.** Nothing to do - you wait for the Belarusian MIA to confirm the authenticity of the licence. Usually 60-90 days. You can track the status on info-car.pl by PESEL and surname.

**Step 5. Collection.** Once confirmation arrives, the urząd prepares the plastic card. You collect the Polish licence in person, free of charge. The original Belarusian licence is sent back to Belarus. The Polish licence is valid for 30 years for cat. A, B (5 years for C, D).

## Can You Drive During the Wait

**Legally - no.** From the moment of złożenie wniosku (submitting the application) until you collect the Polish licence, your Belarusian licence is formally accepted by the urząd. All you hold is the zaświadczenie of submission - this is not a driving document. Driving during this period is an offence carrying a fine of 200-2,000 PLN and the risk of a temporary restriction of your driving entitlement.

There is no "PESEL UKR-style" exception for Belarusians. So plan 2-3 months without a personal car in advance. Realistic alternatives for that period:

1. **Car-sharing** (Traficar, Vozilla) - you need a PESEL + a Polish bank card
2. **Public transport** - a ZTM season ticket across Warsaw
3. **An arrangement with your employer** - if your work depends on a car

Tip: book your urząd visit for a date after which you have a buffer of car-free time - the submission itself takes about 30 minutes, but the wait then stretches to 2-3 months.

## Expired or Lost Licences

**Expired Belarusian licences.** Poland will not accept a document with an expired term for exchange. First renew the licence - and this is harder than for those who renew at home: it usually requires contacting the Belarusian consulate or a trip. Only after renewal do you apply for the exchange. With an expired licence, badania may additionally be required.

**A lost original.** In Poland you cannot exchange a licence on the basis of a copy or duplicate alone, without the original. First restore the original in Belarus (via the consulate or a trip), then begin the exchange. The alternative is to sit the exams at WORD as a "new candidate": faster, but more expensive and without credit for your driving experience - only the category you pass will be opened.

## When It Is Worth Turning to a Lawyer

You can complete the process on your own if you have a new plastic ID-1 licence, no problems with the meldunek, and time for a translator, booking, and a visit to the urząd.

A lawyer makes sense when:

- You have an older-format licence and there is a risk of a request for apostille / notarised confirmation
- The Belarusian MIA does not confirm issuance or the case "hangs" longer than normal
- The name on the licence does not match the passport (name change, transliteration discrepancies)
- There was a refusal by the urząd in the past
- You need Kod 95 in parallel - the two processes can be combined and save 2-3 months
- You want to handle the exchange remotely, via a pełnomocnictwo (power of attorney)
