# Apostille on a Criminal Record Certificate (KRK) for Use Abroad

If a Polish certificate of no criminal record (zaświadczenie o niekaralności, an extract from the KRK) is going abroad - for a US visa, Canadian PR, a UK skilled worker route, or UAE residence - it has to be legalised with an apostille. The apostille is affixed by the Ministerstwo Spraw Zagranicznych (MSZ, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs): the fee is 60 PLN, the term usually 3-5 days. The order is strict: first a fresh KRK, then the apostille, and only then a sworn translation into the destination language. For EU countries, an apostille on the Polish KRK has not been needed at all since 2019.

## Who needs an apostille on the KRK and why

Inside Poland an apostille on the certificate is not required - foreign authorities ask for it when you use a Polish document abroad. The apostille is affixed by the MSZ for 60 PLN, usually in 3-5 days. The correct order: obtain a fresh KRK, get the apostille at the MSZ, then do the sworn translation. For EU countries the apostille is unnecessary under Regulation 2016/1191.

## Where an apostille on the KRK is most often required

A certificate of no criminal record with an apostille is a standard requirement for immigration and long-term visas. The most common destinations:

| Country / procedure | Apostille | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| USA - O-1, EB visas, green card | yes | sworn into English |
| Canada - PR, express entry | yes | certified into English/French |
| UK - skilled worker | yes | into English |
| UAE - residence, work permit | yes + consular legalisation | into English/Arabic |
| Australia - visas, registration | yes | into English |
| EU countries | no (Reg. 2016/1191) | multilingual form / translation |

For most non-European countries you need both an apostille and a translation. For the UAE, beyond the apostille, consular legalisation is often also required - a separate step.

## The correct order: certificate - apostille - translation

This is where people lose money and time. The sequence is rigid:

1. **A fresh KRK.** You obtain the certificate from the Krajowy Rejestr Karny for a specific date - the receiving side's validity window is often 3-6 months.
2. **Apostille at the MSZ.** You affix the apostille on the certificate itself: 60 PLN, usually 3-5 days; for urgent cases, the express tariff (+50%).
3. **Sworn translation.** You translate the finished document together with the text of the apostille itself.

Why exactly this way: the apostille is a stamp with its own text and number. If you translate the certificate before the apostille, the stamp ends up without a translation and you will translate again. So the translation is always the last step.

## Electronic KRK or paper - which to apostille

The KRK comes in two formats, and for the apostille this matters. The electronic e-KRK is a file carrying the Ministry's qualified signature; its legal force lies in the signature inside the file. A paper certificate is an original with a stamp. The MSZ affixes the apostille on a paper document, so for foreign procedures you usually take a paper KRK or make a certified printout of it for the apostille. If you only have the e-KRK, we select the correct format for the requirements of the specific country so the apostille is accepted.

## The EU exception - no apostille needed

If the certificate is going to another European Union country, an apostille on the Polish KRK has not been needed since February 2019 - under Regulation (EU) 2016/1191. The certificate itself is enough, accompanied by a multilingual standard form or a sworn translation. This saves both the 60 PLN and a few days. Important: the exception works only within the EU. For the USA, Canada, the UK and the UAE the apostille is mandatory.

## Validity windows - apostille right after issuance

The KRK is valid for 6 months by default, but the receiving country often looks at the document's "age" more strictly - 3 months for immigration cases, for example. The apostille itself is open-ended, but it cannot "reset" the certificate's age. So the apostille is affixed right after obtaining a fresh KRK, not on a certificate that has been sitting for half a year. If the certificate has "aged", it is simpler to order a new one than to risk a refusal abroad.

## The most common mistakes

**1. Translation before the apostille.** You translated the certificate, then affixed the apostille - and the stamp text is left untranslated. Translate again. Order: apostille, then translation.

**2. Apostille on an expired certificate.** The receiving country looks at the KRK's issue date, not the apostille date. Take a fresh certificate for the filing date.

**3. Apostille for an EU country.** An extra step and extra money - within the EU it is not needed. Check the specific country's requirements in advance.

**4. Mixing up the bodies.** On the KRK the apostille is affixed by the MSZ. This is not a diploma (NAWA there) nor a USC certificate (the wojewoda there).

**5. Only the e-KRK for a paper procedure.** A foreign body may require a paper original with an apostille. Match the format to the country in advance.
