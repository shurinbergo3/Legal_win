# Electronic documents and the apostille: what Poland accepts in 2026

Poland has digitised almost everything in a few short years: company register extracts, criminal record certificates, social security and tax documents, land registers, population register data. Domestically it works beautifully - a file bearing the authority's qualified seal has the force of a document.

The gap opens at the border. **The MSZ issues an apostille only on a paper original with a genuine signature and seal.** A file, however lawful, cannot be apostilled, and neither can a printout of it.

Below, each document type, plus two workarounds.

## The decision table

| Document | How it is issued | What to do before the apostille |
|---|---|---|
| KRS company extract | electronically and on paper at court | order the paper extract from the registry court |
| CEIDG printout | electronically only | notarised printout |
| Criminal record certificate | e-KRK or paper at an information point | order the paper version from the start |
| Tax clearance certificate | e-tax office and paper | request the paper version |
| ZUS certificate | PUE ZUS and paper | request the paper version |
| Land register extract | electronically and at court | order from the land register division |
| Civil registry extract | electronically and on paper | order the paper version |
| Registration certificate | electronically and on paper | order the paper version |
| Financial statements | electronically only (KRS repository) | notarised printout |

The general rule: **if a paper version exists, order it**. Cheaper and faster than the notary route.

## Route one: the paper original

The simplest and cheapest. You apply to the issuing body for a paper version with signature and seal.

The price gap is small:

- current KRS extract - PLN 30 on paper against a free printout;
- criminal record certificate - PLN 30 on paper against PLN 20 online;
- land register extract - PLN 30-60 depending on type;
- civil registry extract - PLN 22-33.

Timeline: one day to a week. From there the document follows the usual chain - an intermediate authority where needed, then the MSZ at PLN 60. [How the chain is built](/en/blog/apostille-ili-legalizacja-konsularna-2026).

## Route two: the notary

For documents issued digitally only - CEIDG, financial statements, some extracts - the notarial route remains.

The notary certifies that the printout matches the electronic document. Then:

1. **Notary** - certification, PLN 20-50 plus VAT.
2. **Regional court** - certification of the notary's signature, PLN 26.
3. **MSZ** - apostille, PLN 60.

Three authorities instead of one, five to ten working days instead of two. But it always works, including where the issuing body refuses paper on principle.

One nuance: what is certified is the **conformity of the printout with the electronic document**, not a "copy of the original". The wording of the notarial clause matters to the receiving side and is worth agreeing in advance.

## Does e-Apostille exist?

The Hague Conference has promoted the e-APP programme for years, covering electronic apostilles and online registers. Some countries already issue them.

Poland does **not issue electronic apostilles** in 2026. The MSZ works on paper and affixes a physical clause. Two consequences follow:

- do not wait for a digital solution - plan the paper route;
- if a foreign counterparty sends you an e-apostille on their own document, that is fine: the issuing country may use the electronic format, and it is verified through that country's online register.

## Three mistakes that cost a week

**Printing it out and taking it to the MSZ.** It comes straight back - no signature, no seal.

**Certifying a copy instead of a printout.** A notary either certifies a copy of a paper original or the conformity of a printout with an electronic document. Digital documents need the second one.

**Translating before the apostille.** The apostille goes on the original; the translation follows and includes the clause - [the full list of refusal reasons](/en/blog/pochemu-otkazyvayut-v-apostile-2026).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is an electronic KRS extract valid in Poland?**
Entirely. The problem only arises when the document travels abroad.

**Can a document signed via ePUAP be apostilled?**
Not directly; through a notarised printout, yes.

**What does the whole notarial route cost?**
Roughly PLN 110-140 in official fees per document - notary, court, MSZ - plus translation.

**Do foreign banks accept Polish electronic documents without an apostille?**
Within the EU, often yes. Outside it, an apostilled paper document is almost always required.

**Which is faster, a paper extract or the notary?**
Paper, if the authority issues it within one to three days. The notarial route wins when the court is backed up.

**Can a representative handle all of it?**
Yes - ordering the documents, the notarial certification under a power of attorney, and filing at the MSZ.
