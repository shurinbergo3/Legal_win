# Exchanging a Ukrainian Driving Licence for a Polish One in 2026: No Exam

If you live in Poland for more than 185 days a year, sooner or later you will have to exchange your Ukrainian driving licence for a Polish one. The good news: for Ukrainian citizens this is the simplest of all post-Soviet cases. Ukraine ratified the 1968 Vienna Convention on Road Traffic, so category A and B licences are exchanged without a theory or practical exam, and Ukraine's Ministry of Internal Affairs answers the Polish office's request faster than its regional neighbours. Below we cover the procedure for Warsaw in 2026: what to do with PESEL UKR, how old and new Ukrainian licences differ, how much it costs, and how not to be left without a car during the wait. We described the general exchange logic for all nationalities in the [pillar article on licence exchange](/en/blog/obmen-prav-na-polskie-2026) - here we go deeper into the Ukraine-specific points.

## In short: what you need to know in 30 seconds

- **No exams** for categories A and B (Vienna Convention). For C, D, C+E, D+E you additionally need Kod 95 and medical tests.
- **When the exchange is mandatory:** living in Poland more than 185 days a year - the Ukrainian licence loses validity for driving.
- **PESEL UKR exception:** the Ukrainian licence stays valid for driving until the end of temporary protection (current horizon - March 2027). But to work as a driver (courier, taxi) a Polish licence is always required.
- **Fee:** 100.00 PLN opłata komunikacyjna + 0.50 PLN opłata ewidencyjna = 100.50 PLN.
- **Timeline:** Ukraine's MIA usually replies within 30-60 days - the fastest channel among CIS countries.
- **Where:** the Wydział Komunikacji (transport and licence registration department) of the Urząd Dzielnicy your meldunek belongs to.
- **Validity of the Polish licence after exchange:** 30 years for A/B, 5 years for C/D.

## Who must exchange and when

Under Polish law (Ustawa o kierujących pojazdami, art. 14) any person with their actual place of residence in Poland for **more than 185 days in a calendar year** must hold a Polish prawo jazdy (driving licence). A foreign licence loses its legal force for driving - even if it is formally "recognised" under the Vienna Convention.

The 185 days are counted per calendar year. Proof of residence includes a meldunek (address registration), a tenancy agreement, a ZUS entry, an employer's oświadczenie (statement). If you arrived on a D visa, obtained a karta pobytu, or received a PESEL UKR and actually live in Poland - the clock has started.

For Ukrainians there is an important relief here that Belarusians and other CIS nationals do not have. We cover it separately below, because PESEL UKR is where most of the confusion arises.

## Are exams required for the exchange

For categories **A and B - no.** Ukraine is a party to the 1968 Vienna Convention, so the Polish office exchanges the licence without a theory or practical exam. Confirmation of authenticity from Ukraine's MIA is enough - and the Polish plastic card is ready.

The exception is the professional categories **C, D, C+E, D+E.** The exchange itself still happens without exams, but to carry goods or passengers for a fee you will additionally need:

- valid badania lekarskie (medical examination) from a lekarz uprawniony (authorised doctor) for drivers;
- valid badania psychologiczne (psychological examination);
- a Kod 95 certificate - the professional driver qualification. We explain how to obtain it in detail in the [Kod 95 guide](/en/blog/kod-95-polsha-kak-poluchit).

So your category C licence will be exchanged even without Kod 95, but you cannot work as a professional driver without it.

## The Ukraine-specific angle: PESEL UKR and the fast MIA channel

Two things set the Ukrainian case apart from all others.

**First - the PESEL UKR exception.** A Ukrainian licence stays valid for driving until the end of temporary protection (the special UKR status). The current horizon is March 2027. This means that formally you may drive on your Ukrainian licence without exchanging it, as long as the temporary protection status is in force.

But there is a key caveat: this exception applies only to **driving a private vehicle.** If you want to work as a driver - a delivery courier, a taxi driver, a truck driver - a Polish licence is always required, regardless of PESEL UKR. Employers and platforms (Bolt, Uber, courier companies) require precisely a Polish prawo jazdy. That is why most people planning to earn behind the wheel exchange their licence in advance, without waiting for the end of temporary protection.

**Second - speed.** Ukraine's MIA answers the Polish office's request through a direct/online channel (the request goes via the CEPiK system). In practice this is the fastest case among CIS countries: confirmation usually arrives within 30-60 days, whereas for a [Belarusian licence](/en/blog/obmen-belorusskih-prav-na-polskie-2026), for example, the timeline is more often 60-90 days. At this stage you do not need to do anything - the office sends the request, you simply wait.

## Old vs new Ukrainian licences

The licence format affects how smoothly the exchange goes.

| Licence format | How it exchanges | What may be additionally required |
|---|---|---|
| New ID-1 plastic (recent years) | Smoothly, no questions | Translation and the basic package only |
| Old paper / laminated (Soviet-era or 1990s) | Exchanges, but the office may request confirmation of issuance | Additional confirmation from Ukraine if in doubt |

New ID-1 plastic licences are exchanged without issues - they are read well by the system and raise no questions. Old paper or laminated licences of the Soviet pattern or from the 1990s sometimes trigger a request for additional confirmation of issuance: Ukraine's MIA must confirm that such a licence was indeed issued. This is not a refusal but a formal check, though it can add a few weeks to the timeline.

A separate note on the digital format: the electronic licence in the "Diia" app on its own is **not enough** for the exchange. The Polish office needs the physical original - the plastic or paper document. Showing the e-licence on a phone screen when submitting the wniosek will not work - it is the original they take.

## Where to exchange in Warsaw

Exchanging a Ukrainian driving licence in Warsaw takes place at the Wydział Komunikacji of the Urząd Dzielnicy your meldunek belongs to:

- Śródmieście - ul. Nowogrodzka 43
- Mokotów - ul. Rakowiecka 25/27
- Ursynów - al. Komisji Edukacji Narodowej 61
- Wola - al. Solidarności 90
- Praga-Południe - ul. Grochowska 274
- Bemowo - ul. Powstańców Śląskich 70

Booking is via the warszawa19115.pl portal or the Warszawa 19115 app (section "Wydział Komunikacji" → "Wymiana zagranicznego prawa jazdy"). In the hot season (September-October, April-May) you need to book 3 weeks ahead - that is when demand peaks. If your district has no free slots, it is worth checking a neighbouring office, but you must still submit according to your meldunek.

## Documents - the full list

**Mandatory for everyone:**

- Wniosek o wydanie prawa jazdy - a completed application form in Polish (the form is issued at the counter or downloadable from the office website)
- Ukrainian licence - original + copy of both sides
- Tłumaczenie przysięgłe (sworn translation) of the licence into Polish from Ukrainian - cost 60-80 PLN, ready in 1-3 days
- Passport - original + copy of the photo page
- A document confirming legal pobyt (residence): karta pobytu, D visa, or PESEL UKR + meldunek
- Photo 35 × 45 mm - front-facing, no headwear, no older than 6 months
- Proof of payment: 100.00 PLN opłata komunikacyjna + 0.50 PLN opłata ewidencyjna

**Additionally for categories C, D, C+E, D+E:**

- Valid badania lekarskie from a lekarz uprawniony - 180-250 PLN
- Valid badania psychologiczne - 130-180 PLN
- A Kod 95 certificate - if you plan to work as a professional driver

**If you changed your surname:**

- Marriage or divorce certificate with a tłumaczenie przysięgłe
- The old document with the old surname, if available

## Step by step

**Step 1. Translation.** You go to a tłumacz przysięgły (sworn translator) from Ukrainian (the directory is at TEPIS.org.pl). You hand over the licence + a copy of your passport and receive the tłumaczenie przysięgłe in 1-3 working days.

**Step 2. Booking.** Via warszawa19115.pl you select the Wydział Komunikacji of your district → the service "Wymiana zagranicznego prawa jazdy" → a free slot.

**Step 3. The office visit.** You bring all documents. The clerk accepts the wniosek, takes the original licence, issues a zaświadczenie (a receipt certificate), creates a PKK (Profil Kandydata na Kierowcę - the electronic case card), and immediately sends a request to Ukraine's MIA via the CEPiK system.

**Step 4. The wait.** You do not need to do anything - you wait for confirmation from Ukraine's MIA. Usually 30-60 days. You can track the status on info-car.pl by PESEL and surname.

**Step 5. Collection.** You collect the Polish licence in person, free of charge. The Ukrainian original is not returned to you - it is sent back to Ukraine. The Polish licence is valid for 30 years for categories A/B (5 years for C/D).

## Can you drive while waiting

This is our clients' most frequent question, and there is a trap here.

**As a general rule - no.** From the moment the wniosek is submitted until you collect the Polish licence, your Ukrainian original has already been accepted by the office. All you hold is the zaświadczenie of submission, which by itself is not a driving document. Driving during this period formally counts as driving without a valid licence.

**The exception for PESEL UKR holders.** If you have valid temporary protection, the Ukrainian licence stays valid for driving even during the exchange. In practice, if the police stop you, show both documents: the Ukrainian licence (or confirmation of its validity) and the zaświadczenie of the exchange submission. More on timelines and fines for driving without valid documents - in the [article on the 185-day rule](/en/blog/pravilo-185-dney-itd-shtrafy).

## Expired or lost licences

**Expired Ukrainian licence.** Poland does not exchange an expired document. First you have to renew it - via the Ukrainian consulate; some renewals are also possible from Ukraine. Only once validity is restored can you apply for the exchange.

**Lost original.** Without the original the exchange is impossible - a copy or the electronic version in "Diia" is not enough. There are two options: first restore the original in Ukraine (via the consulate or a trip), then exchange it, or sit the exams at WORD as a new candidate. The second path is faster on timing but more expensive, and it does not account for your experience - only the category you pass will be opened. More on the exam route - in the [article on exchanging US, UK, and Indian licences via WORD](/en/blog/zamena-prav-ssha-uk-india-word-ekzamen).

## When it is worth turning to a lawyer

You can go through the process yourself in one visit to a translator and one visit to the office - especially if you have a new plastic licence and no problems with your meldunek.

A lawyer makes sense if:

- you have an old paper licence and the office demands additional confirmation of issuance;
- you changed your surname and there are mismatches between the licence and the passport;
- you also need Kod 95 - that process is several steps longer, and it is sensible to run both matters together;
- there was a prior refusal by the office;
- you want to arrange the exchange remotely, without in-person visits (via pełnomocnictwo - power of attorney).
