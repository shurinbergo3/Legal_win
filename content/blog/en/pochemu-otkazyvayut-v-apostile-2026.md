# Why the Polish MFA turns documents away: 9 reasons an apostille fails

An apostille in Poland looks simple: PLN 60, the MSZ, a stamp. In practice one filing in five comes back - and almost always for one of nine reasons that have repeated for years.

Here they are in order of frequency, so you do not lose a week on a second attempt.

## 1. A copy instead of an original

The apostille attaches to a document bearing a genuine signature and seal. A photocopy, a scan, a "duplicate" printout or a page from a website will not do.

The fix: obtain the original from the issuing body, or make a **notarised copy** and run it through the Sąd Okręgowy (regional court) - the apostille then certifies the notarial act.

## 2. An electronic document

The most common mistake of 2026. A KRS printout, an e-KRK certificate, an ePUAP document, an electronic civil registry extract - all carry full force inside Poland, and none carries a physical signature or seal.

The MSZ will not take such printouts directly. The answer is a paper original, or notarisation of the printout. [Full breakdown of electronic documents](/en/blog/e-apostille-elektronnye-dokumenty-2026).

## 3. A skipped intermediate authority

Not every document goes straight to the MSZ:

| Document type | Certified before the MSZ by |
|---|---|
| Civil registry certificates (birth, marriage, death) | wojewoda |
| Notarial deeds and powers of attorney | regional court |
| University diplomas | NAWA |
| School certificates and course diplomas | education authority |
| Medical records | often the voivodeship inspectorate |
| Criminal record certificate, KRS extract | MSZ directly |

Bring a birth certificate straight to the MSZ and it goes back with a pointer to the wojewoda, costing another two to five days.

## 4. A document outside the Convention's scope

The 1961 Hague Convention expressly excludes two categories: documents executed by diplomatic or consular agents, and administrative documents **dealing directly with commercial or customs operations**.

In practice: invoices, waybills, certificates of origin and customs declarations cannot be apostilled. They run through a different mechanism - certification by the Polish Chamber of Commerce and the consulate.

The trap usually comes from the counterparty asking for an "apostilled invoice" without knowing the exclusion. Clarify the requirement before going to the MSZ.

## 5. No specimen signature on file

The MSZ compares the official's signature against deposited specimens. A new official whose specimen has not been filed, an old-format seal, or a document from a body long since abolished means refusal or a long verification.

The fix: a current document from an operating authority; for archival documents, prior notarisation sometimes helps.

## 6. The apostille went on the translation

The classic sequencing error. The correct order is **original, apostille, sworn translation including the apostille clause**.

Translate first and either the clause goes untranslated, or the translation has to be redone once the apostille exists.

The exception: some countries want an apostille on both the original and the translation. Confirm that with the recipient in advance rather than guessing.

## 7. Lamination, damage, handwritten additions

A laminated document counts as altered - the clause cannot be attached and the substrate cannot be verified. The same applies to erasures, handwritten notes, water damage, tears and tape repairs.

The rule: **never laminate a document that might travel abroad**. Recovery means ordering a fresh copy.

## 8. Incorrect payment

The fee is PLN 60 per document, not per bundle, paid to the Śródmieście District Office account. Common errors: a single lump payment for five documents with no breakdown, the wrong payee, and no payment confirmation in the pack.

Consular legalisation - for countries outside the Convention - carries a different rate of PLN 26. [We compare the two routes separately](/en/blog/apostille-ili-legalizacja-konsularna-2026).

## 9. The document is stale for the recipient

Technically this is not an MSZ refusal - it will apostille a document of any age. The refusal arrives later, abroad.

Freshness benchmarks:

- **criminal record certificate** - three months, sometimes six;
- **KRS extract** - three months for banks, six for tenders;
- **civil registry certificates** - formally open-ended, but recipients often want one issued within three to six months;
- **income and tax clearance certificates** - one to three months.

The apostille itself **never expires** - the limit always comes from the document.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can an MSZ refusal be appealed?**
Rarely worth it - the refusals are formal, and fixing the cause is faster.

**How many apostilles can one document carry?**
One per copy. Three destination countries means three originals.

**Can a foreign document be apostilled in Poland?**
No, only by the issuing country - [how to arrange it remotely](/en/blog/apostille-inostrannyh-dokumentov-dlya-polshi-2026).

**What about an old Soviet-era certificate?**
Usually a fresh extract must be issued by the archive of the country holding the original record; the apostille then goes on the new document.

**Will a Polish-language apostille be recognised abroad?**
Yes. The clause follows a standard form with numbered fields identical across countries. The document itself normally needs translating.
