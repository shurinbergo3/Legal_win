# Register sp. z o.o. via S24 without PESEL in 2026: how a foreigner gets to the finish line

Registering a sp. z o.o. with no PESEL is where most foreigners get stuck. You find the S24 system, open the form, fill everything in, reach the point where you have to sign the application - and hit a wall. Profil Zaufany (Trusted Profile), the tool Poles click through in one second, is closed to you, because it needs a PESEL. And without a signature, the application to KRS (Krajowy Rejestr Sądowy - the national court register of companies) simply never leaves your screen.

The good news: there is a way through, and more than one. Below we explain why the usual path breaks down without a PESEL, which two options actually work, what the capital and state fees add up to, and what you have to get done in the first two weeks after registration so you do not collect a fine on day one.

---

## Can you register a sp. z o.o. without a PESEL

Yes. A foreigner with no PESEL can open a sp. z o.o. in Poland. A PESEL is not a condition for being an udziałowiec (shareholder) or a member of the zarząd (management board). The only real obstacle is how you sign the application to KRS: instead of Profil Zaufany, which requires a PESEL, you need a podpis kwalifikowany (qualified electronic signature) or registration in front of a notary.

This is the point that trips people up. Your right to own a company exists from day one, whether you already hold a karta pobytu (residence card) or you are still abroad. The limitation is purely technical: what you sign the documents with.

### Why Profil Zaufany does not work without a PESEL

S24 is the online system run by the Ministry of Justice for fast registration of companies using a ready-made template of the umowa spółki (articles of association). To file the application, every udziałowiec and every board member has to sign it electronically. Poles, and foreigners who already have a PESEL, do this through Profil Zaufany (ePUAP), a free government tool.

But Profil Zaufany is tied to the PESEL number. No PESEL means no profile, which means you cannot sign the application this way. That is exactly where people freeze after spending an evening filling in the form, convinced they were two clicks from done.

In Mazowieckie we see the same story on repeat: the client works through S24 alone, gets to the signature, and then writes to us asking "why won't it submit." The answer is always the same - you need a different signing tool.

## Two working options for a foreigner without a PESEL

It comes down to two routes. Both are legal and both end in the same place: a company in KRS. What differs is price, speed, and whether you have to show up in person.

### Option 1: podpis kwalifikowany (qualified electronic signature)

This is an electronic signature issued by commercial certification providers in Poland and across the EU. It fully replaces Profil Zaufany inside S24. A foreigner can obtain one without a PESEL, because the signature is issued against a passport.

Upside: once you hold it, you register the company entirely online, from anywhere, in one or two working days. The signature stays with you and keeps earning its keep afterwards - for CRBR, for signing documents at the tax office, for e-invoices.

Downside: it costs money (usually between 300 and 500 PLN for one or two years) and setting it up takes a little time, since it often requires video verification or in-person identity checks.

### Option 2: registration through a notary (akt notarialny)

The classic route. The umowa spółki is concluded as a notarial deed, and the notary files the application with KRS. No electronic signature is needed - a passport and your physical presence at the notary are enough.

Upside: no wrestling with an e-signature, the notary checks the documents, and you can step outside the S24 template to write non-standard terms into the umowa spółki.

Downside: it costs more. You pay the notary's fee plus a tłumacz przysięgły (sworn translator) if you do not speak Polish, since the notary must be satisfied that you understand what you are signing. And you have to appear in person.

### Which one to pick

| Criterion | S24 + podpis kwalifikowany | Notary |
|-----------|---------------------------|--------|
| In-person visit needed | No, fully online | Yes, in person at the notary |
| Signature/fee cost | ~300-500 PLN for the signature | Notary fee + translator |
| Registration speed | 1-2 days | Depends on the notary |
| Flexibility of umowa spółki | S24 template only | Any terms |
| Reusable afterwards | Yes, the signature stays | No |

Our usual advice: if you plan to run the company actively and sign documents online, get the podpis kwalifikowany - it pays for itself. If you need a non-standard umowa spółki, or you simply do not want to deal with electronics, go to the notary.

## Capital and the structure of the umowa spółki

The minimum kapitał zakładowy (share capital) for a sp. z o.o. is 5 000 PLN. This is not a deposit you have to freeze in an account: the capital goes into the company and is used in its operations. The minimum value of a single udział (share) is 50 PLN, so a capital of 5 000 PLN gives you 100 shares at 50 PLN each.

The umowa spółki has to set out:

- **siedziba** - the registered office of the company (city or locality);
- **przedmiot działalności** - the scope of business, given as PKD codes;
- **kapitał zakładowy** - the amount of capital and the number of shares;
- **udziały** - how the capital is split between shareholders;
- **reprezentacja** - who represents the company and how (for example, each board member acting alone).

### PKD 2025: business activity codes

Activities are stated as PKD codes, and since 2025 a new classification applies: PKD 2025. If you copy old codes from a guide written three years ago, the system may reject them or they may simply be wrong.

A classic mistake our clients make is to pick one narrow code "just in case" and later discover the real business does not fall under it. State a main code plus a few additional ones that cover what the company will actually do. You can expand the list later, but that means an extra change filed with KRS.

## State costs of registering through S24

The state fees for S24 registration are small and predictable:

| Payment | Amount |
|---------|--------|
| Opłata sądowa (court fee) | 250 PLN |
| PCC (tax on the umowa spółki), 0.5% | ~24 PLN on capital of 5 000 PLN |
| Publication in MSiG (Monitor Sądowy i Gospodarczy) | ~100 PLN |
| **Total state fees** | **~275-350 PLN** |

PCC (podatek od czynności cywilnoprawnych - tax on civil law transactions) is charged at 0.5% of a base equal to the capital minus the court fee and the publication cost. On the minimum capital of 5 000 PLN that comes to a symbolic ~24 PLN, and you have to declare and pay it yourself within 14 days.

On top of the state fees, add the cost of your chosen signing method: either the podpis kwalifikowany (~300-500 PLN) or the notary and translator fees.

## What to do straight after KRS registration

Getting a KRS number is only half the job. What follows are mandatory steps with hard deadlines, and missing them here costs money. This is exactly the stage where things tend to go wrong for people who registered the company on their own.

**1. Report data to the tax office within 7 days (NIP-8).** After KRS registration the company automatically receives a NIP and a REGON, but additional data (bank account number, where documents are kept, and so on) must be filed with the urząd skarbowy (tax office) on form NIP-8 within 7 days.

**2. Choose your tax form.** At the same time you decide on the form of profit tax and whether you will be a VAT payer. That choice shapes all of your future bookkeeping, so it is not one to make blindly.

**3. CRBR within 14 days.** You have to report the beneficjent rzeczywisty (beneficial owner) to the Centralny Rejestr Beneficjentów Rzeczywistych within 14 days of KRS registration. It is a separate obligation many people forget, and the fines are serious - we cover them in detail in a separate article.

**4. Bank account.** You need a company account to operate. Opening one is harder for a foreigner than for a Pole, as banks treat non-residents differently, and there are nuances worth knowing in advance.

The post-KRS deadlines look like this:

| Action | Deadline |
|--------|----------|
| NIP-8 to the tax office | 7 days |
| Choice of taxation | When filing NIP-8 |
| CRBR - beneficjent rzeczywisty | 14 days |
| PCC (declaration and payment) | 14 days |

## How long registration takes

Counting from the moment you have a signing tool in hand:

- **Through S24 with a podpis kwalifikowany** - the application is usually reviewed in 1-2 working days, sometimes the same day.
- **Through a notary** - it depends on the notary's and the court's workload; on average from a few days to a couple of weeks.

The real time sink is not the registration itself but the preparation: getting the signature, agreeing the umowa spółki, choosing PKD codes, deciding on tax. Once everything is ready, the company appears in KRS quickly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I register a sp. z o.o. from abroad without coming to Poland?**
Yes, if you use a podpis kwalifikowany - the whole S24 procedure runs online and no physical visit is required. Registration through a notary does require you to be present, so that route will not work remotely.

**Do I have to get a PESEL to open a company?**
No. A PESEL is needed neither to be an udziałowiec nor to sit on the zarząd. It is only needed for Profil Zaufany, and that is replaced by a podpis kwalifikowany or notary registration.

**How much money do I really need to start?**
The minimum kapitał zakładowy of 5 000 PLN, plus state fees of ~275-350 PLN, plus the cost of the signature (~300-500 PLN) or the notary fees. The capital stays inside the company and funds its activity - it does not disappear.

**What happens if I miss the 14-day CRBR deadline?**
Late filing of beneficial owner data in CRBR carries a fine of up to 1 000 000 PLN. It is one of the most common deadlines missed by people who registered a company themselves, so put it in your calendar right away.

**Can I do S24 myself and arrange the signature separately?**
Technically yes, but in practice people get stuck exactly at "the form is ready but the signature does not work." If you go it alone, get the signing tool first and only then fill in the application, or you risk hitting a wall at the final step.
