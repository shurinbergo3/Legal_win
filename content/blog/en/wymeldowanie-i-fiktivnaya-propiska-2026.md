# Deregistration in Poland: how to do it and why fake addresses backfire

Registering an address in Poland takes fifteen minutes. Deregistering is sometimes just as quick and sometimes turns into a three-month administrative case. It depends on who starts it and whether the other side cooperates.

Three scenarios below: deregistering yourself, an owner removing a tenant who has moved out, and the "registration for a fee" question both sides keep asking.

## When deregistration is actually needed

A separate procedure is required less often than people think:

| Situation | Separate deregistration |
|---|---|
| Moving to another flat in Poland | no - the new notification cancels the old entry |
| Leaving Poland for more than six months | yes, a departure notification |
| A temporary registration reaching its end date | no, the entry lapses |
| A tenant moved out without deregistering | yes, administrative route on the owner's application |
| Selling a flat with people registered in it | usually yes |

Worth remembering: **a new registration cancels the old one by itself**. There is no need for two visits.

## Deregistering yourself

The notification goes to the municipal or district office for the address you are leaving. Bring ID and the completed deregistration form. It is free and recorded the same day. A certificate of deregistration costs PLN 17 if a bank, tax office or foreign authority needs one.

If you are leaving Poland permanently, a separate form applies - notification of departure abroad. That matters for tax: deregistration together with a notice to the tax office is one of the arguments when changing tax residency - [the 183-day and centre-of-interests mechanics](/en/blog/nalogovoye-rezidentstvo-polsha-183-dnya).

## Removing a tenant who moved out

A familiar situation: the tenant has gone, taken their belongings, and the register entry stayed. They will not deregister themselves, and sometimes they have left the country.

The owner files an **application for deregistration by administrative decision**. The authority runs proceedings, establishes that the person no longer lives there, and issues a decision.

Evidence that works:

- the lease with its end date and the handover protocol;
- correspondence about moving out, including the termination notice;
- statements from neighbours or the building manager;
- no utility consumption on the meters;
- a new tenant living at the address;
- returned keys and a returned deposit.

Timeline: four to twelve weeks depending on the municipality and on whether the person responds to summonses. The decision fee is PLN 10.

The mirror procedure - registering without the owner's consent - works symmetrically, and we cover it [here](/en/blog/zameldowanie-bez-soglasiya-sobstvennika-2026).

## Fictitious registration: risk on both sides

"Register me for 500 zloty" comes up regularly. We do not provide that, and not only for the obvious reason.

**Risk for the person being registered.**
The notification states your actual place of residence. Knowingly giving a false address is not a harmless formality - obtaining an official document on false data falls under the criminal provisions on procuring a false certification.

The practical consequences arrive before the criminal ones. In a residence case the wojewoda verifies where you live, and does so properly: enquiries to the municipality, interviews, cross-checks against employer data. A discovered discrepancy damages not the registration but the credibility of the whole file.

**Risk for the owner.**
Not the one usually feared. A registered person genuinely acquires no rights to the flat. What the owner does acquire:

- someone who will later need removing through administrative proceedings;
- questions from the tax office about rental income if the lease appears nowhere;
- utility charges recalculated by the number of registered occupants;
- potential exposure if the address was used in fraudulent schemes.

The sensible alternative for both sides is the same: **a legal lease with the right to register written into it**. An occasional lease with a notarial submission to enforcement protects the landlord better than refusing to register - [we cover that format separately](/en/blog/arenda-kvartiry-najem-okazjonalny-2026).

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I deregister online?**
Polish and EU citizens can, through gov.pl, with an active eDoręczenia address. Third-country nationals handle registration matters in person or through a representative.

**I was deregistered without my knowledge. What now?**
The decision is appealable: 14 days to the wojewoda, then the administrative court. If you genuinely live there, the odds are good - the authority had to establish the facts.

**Does deregistration affect my residence card?**
Not directly, but the wojewoda sees register data. Deregistering with no new registration raises the question of where you live, and it is better answered in advance.

**Do I need to clear registrations before selling a flat?**
Legally registered occupants do not block a sale, but buyers and banks almost always want a property free of registrations.

**What if the tenant ignores the summonses?**
Non-appearance does not stop the procedure; the authority decides on the evidence.

**What does the administrative route cost?**
PLN 10 for the decision. The real cost is the time spent assembling evidence.
