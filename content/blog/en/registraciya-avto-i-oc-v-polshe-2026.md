# Registering a car in Poland as a foreigner and buying OC in 2026

A foreigner can own a car in Poland and register it in their own name without a residence card. Three things almost always come as a surprise: **the 30-day deadline**, **the fine for missing it**, and **the need for a PESEL number to buy third-party cover**.

Here is the procedure in order, with the points where people lose time and money.

## The 30-day deadline and the fine

The owner must register the vehicle, or notify the authority of the change of owner, within **30 days**:

- from buying a car already registered in Poland;
- from importing a car from abroad.

Missing the deadline carries a **fine of PLN 500 to 1,000**, imposed by the district authority. Enforcement has become routine over recent years.

A separate point for second-hand purchases: without re-registration, camera-issued penalties go to the previous owner, who passes them on to you. And without registration you cannot buy third-party cover in your own name.

## What registration requires

The pack for the vehicle department at the district or city office:

- **proof of ownership** - a sale agreement, invoice or deed of gift;
- **the previous registration certificate** with a valid technical inspection;
- **the vehicle card**, if one was issued;
- **the previous registration plates**;
- a **third-party insurance policy** valid on the filing date;
- **identity documents** - passport plus residence document;
- **proof of address** - which is where registration enters the picture;
- a **power of attorney** if a representative files;
- for imported cars, **excise and customs documents** and sworn translations.

The critical point: **the vehicle department takes the owner's address from the register**. No meldunek, no address, no application - the most common reason foreigners stall at step one. [We complete zameldowanie in one to two days](/en/uslugi/meldunek).

## Importing a car: excise, VAT, customs

**From an EU country.** No customs clearance, but **excise duty** applies: 3.1% of value for engines up to 2,000 cc and 18.6% above that. The AKC-U/S declaration is filed within 14 days of import and paid within 30. Electric vehicles and some hybrids are exempt.

**From outside the EU.** Customs duty, usually 10%, and **23% VAT** are added, both calculated on the customs value, plus excise. The total burden on an import from the US, for instance, often exceeds 40% of the vehicle's value.

After clearance and excise payment comes a mandatory **technical inspection**, and only then registration.

## Third-party cover: why you need a PESEL

Third-party motor cover (OC) is compulsory from the first day of ownership, not from registration. Driving without it triggers a charge from the Insurance Guarantee Fund, calculated per day uninsured and running into thousands of zloty.

The market reality: **insurers issue policies against a PESEL number**. Owner identification in the guarantee fund systems and in pricing is built on it, so "passport only, no PESEL" simply does not work at most insurers - a feature of the system rather than an individual agent's policy.

Hence the correct order for a foreigner:

1. **Address registration**, which triggers a PESEL automatically for stays over 30 days, or a separate PESEL application.
2. **The insurance policy.**
3. **Technical inspection**, where required.
4. **Registration** at the vehicle department.

## What drives the premium

- **Claims history.** No-claims discounts earned abroad are recognised partially or not at all, depending on the insurer.
- **Age and driving experience**, counted from the issue date of your first licence.
- **Registered address.** Warsaw and a small town price very differently.
- **Type of driving licence.** A Polish licence usually prices lower. If you live in Poland for more than 185 days, exchanging your licence is mandatory anyway - [how it works](/en/blog/obmen-prav-na-polskie-2026).

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I register a car without a residence card?**
Yes. Registration needs no residence permit, but it does need an address in the register and a PESEL for insurance.

**How long can I drive on foreign plates?**
A car registered abroad may be used temporarily, but once you move your residence and use it permanently it must be registered. The 30 days run from import.

**What if the car belongs to a company?**
Registration goes to the company and the address comes from the company register, so the owner's personal registration is not needed.

**Does foreign third-party cover apply?**
EU policies and Green Card cover work within their own terms, but once the car is registered in Poland a Polish policy is required.

**Is a foreign technical inspection accepted?**
For EU vehicles it is sometimes recognised, but registration usually requires a Polish inspection. For non-EU imports it is mandatory.

**Can everything be handled remotely?**
Registration, yes, under a power of attorney. The technical inspection, no - the vehicle must physically attend the station.
