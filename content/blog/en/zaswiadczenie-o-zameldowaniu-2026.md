# Certificate of registration in Poland: who asks for it and how to get it

The meldunek is an entry in a register. The zaświadczenie o zameldowaniu is the document that proves it - and it is the document that banks, schools, the vehicle department, insurers, courts and foreign counterparties actually ask for.

It costs PLN 17 for temporary registration and **PLN 0 for permanent**. It is issued the same day. The difficulty is rarely in getting one; it is in knowing which of the several types you need.

## The types of certificate

| Type | What it proves | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Certificate of permanent registration | permanent registration | PLN 0 |
| Certificate of temporary registration | temporary registration and its period | PLN 17 |
| Certificate of deregistration | that you have been removed from an address | PLN 17 |
| Certificate of the number of registered persons | how many people are registered in a flat | PLN 17 |
| Full PESEL register extract | all register data about you | PLN 17 |

The last one is often the rescue: when an authority asks vaguely for "confirmation of your data", the full extract closes the question better than a narrow certificate.

The certificate on the number of registered persons matters to owners - when selling a flat, in disputes with the building association, and in utility settlements.

## Where it is asked for

Our list, by frequency:

1. **Banks** - business accounts, loans, mortgages, AML procedures.
2. **The vehicle department** - registering a car, exchanging a driving licence.
3. **Schools and nurseries** - catchment-area admission.
4. **The wojewoda** - as proof of residence in a residence card application.
5. **The tax office** - determining which office you belong to.
6. **Insurers** - pricing motor cover by address.
7. **Courts** - establishing territorial jurisdiction.
8. **Foreign authorities** - proof of residence, with apostille and translation.

## Four ways to obtain it

**1. In person at the municipal or district office.** ID, application, PLN 17. The document is issued the same day, usually within 10-20 minutes.

**2. Through mObywatel.** The app exposes PESEL register data. The electronic version works in some situations but not all.

**3. Through ePUAP or gov.pl.** An application signed with a trusted profile; the certificate arrives with an electronic seal. Since 2026 some electronic services require an active eDoręczenia address.

**4. Through a representative** under a power of attorney, with PLN 17 stamp duty for the authorisation. That is how we work - no visit, no queue.

## Electronic or paper

An electronic certificate bearing the office's qualified seal carries the same force as paper **inside Poland**. Banks, schools and public offices accept it.

Three situations that need paper:

- **Apostille.** The MSZ handles paper originals only - [we cover that trap separately](/en/blog/e-apostille-elektronnye-dokumenty-2026).
- **Foreign authorities** with no way to verify a Polish electronic seal.
- **Certain banks** whose internal AML procedures demand an original.

## How fresh it has to be

There is no statutory expiry - the certificate reflects the register on the day it was issued. Recipients typically want it no older than **one to three months**; banks often insist on 30 days.

Which means: do not order one "to have on file". It is cheap, and freshness is precisely what gets checked.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can any office issue the certificate?**
No. It comes from the office keeping the register for your registered address. The electronic channel has no such limit.

**I need the certificate urgently but I am not registered. What now?**
Registration first, certificate second - achievable in one day if the owner signs the form. If they refuse, the situation is different: [covered here](/en/blog/zameldowanie-bez-soglasiya-sobstvennika-2026).

**Will a bank accept the mObywatel extract?**
Most will, though branch policy varies, so for banking matters we usually collect the paper version.

**Is the certificate required for a residence card application?**
The wojewoda wants proof of where you live. A lease can work; the certificate is safer. A scan is attached when filing through MOS v2.0.

**How much is a certificate for a child?**
The same. A legal representative applies, presenting the birth certificate.

**Can I get a certificate about past registration?**
Yes - the office issues confirmation of historical registration. It is often needed for permanent residence applications where continuity of stay is counted.
