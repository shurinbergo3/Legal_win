# Exchanging a Russian Driving Licence for a Polish One in 2026

If you live in Poland for more than 185 days a year, your Russian driving licence loses validity for driving, and the law requires you to exchange it for a Polish one. The good news: Russia is a party to the 1968 Vienna Convention on Road Traffic, so for categories A and B the exchange happens without a theory or practical exam — on the same legal basis as Ukrainian and Belarusian licences. There is one difficulty, and it is purely technical: confirming the licence's authenticity takes longer than for neighbouring countries. Below we walk through the Warsaw procedure, the realistic timelines, and the verification nuance that drives all the planning.

## In brief: what to know in 30 seconds

- **No exams** for cat. A and B (Vienna Convention); for C, D, C+E, D+E you need Kod 95 + badania
- **When you must exchange:** after 185 days of residence in Poland in a calendar year
- **Where to do it:** the Wydział Komunikacji (transport department) of the Urząd Dzielnicy that covers your meldunek (registered address)
- **What it costs:** 100.50 PLN in official fees + 70-110 PLN tłumaczenie przysięgłe (sworn translation); total for cat. B about 180-270 PLN
- **How long it takes:** 90-120+ days — there is no direct electronic verification channel with Russia, so the request goes through a consular route
- **Will they take the original:** yes, it is sent back to the issuing country
- **Polish licence validity:** 30 years for A, B (5 years for C, D)

## Who must exchange, and when

Under Polish law (Ustawa o kierujących pojazdami, art. 14 — the act on vehicle drivers), anyone whose actual place of residence is in Poland for **more than 185 days in a calendar year** must hold a Polish prawo jazdy (driving licence). A foreign licence then loses its legal force for driving — even if it is "recognised" under the letter of the convention.

The 185 days are counted per calendar year. Proof includes a meldunek, a rental agreement, ZUS registration, or an employer's oświadczenie (declaration). Late exchange carries a fine of 200 to 2,000 PLN for driving without valid documents; after a collision the insurer may refuse to pay out.

Russian nationals who arrived on a karta pobytu (residence card) for work, business, or family especially often miss this deadline: a Russian licence can be valid for up to 10 years, so it feels like exchange isn't necessary. Legally, it is.

## Are exams required

For categories **A and B, no exams are needed**. Russia ratified the 1968 Vienna Convention on Road Traffic, and on that basis a Russian licence is directly exchangeable without theory or practice — exactly like Ukrainian and Belarusian licences. This is the key difference from countries outside the convention (e.g. the USA, the UK, India), where you have to [sit the WORD exam as a new candidate](/en/blog/zamena-prav-ssha-uk-india-word-ekzamen).

For **professional categories C, D, C+E, D+E**, exchange alone is not enough. You will additionally need:

- valid badania lekarskie (medical examination) from a lekarz uprawniony — a physician authorised to examine drivers;
- valid badania psychologiczne (psychological examination);
- a **Kod 95** certificate to work as a professional driver — a separate course and procedure (see our [Kod 95 guide](/en/blog/kod-95-polsha-kak-poluchit)).

## The Russian-licence specific: verification and timelines

This is the main difference from the Ukrainian and Belarusian cases, and it is purely technical.

When you submit the wniosek (application), the urząd must confirm with the issuing country that the licence was genuinely issued to you and has not been revoked. For Ukraine and Belarus that request partly runs over working channels, and the answer arrives within 30-90 days. **The Polish CEPiK system has no direct electronic verification channel with Russia** — the request to confirm issuance goes through a consular (diplomatic) route. So verification is **slower and less predictable**: realistically 90-120+ days, sometimes longer.

A second practical consequence follows: because there is no direct registry answer, the urząd **more often asks for additional proof** of the licence's authenticity. This may be an apostille or a notarised confirmation of the document's authenticity — especially for older-format licences. It is better to plan for these documents in advance rather than wait for the official's request, otherwise you lose several more weeks.

It is precisely because of this slower, less certain verification that engaging a lawyer or acting via pełnomocnictwo (power of attorney) is justified more often for Russian licences than for Ukrainian or Belarusian ones. A power of attorney lets the case proceed without you being present in person and lets you respond quickly to the urząd's requests.

## Old vs new Russian licences

The document format affects the risk of an additional request:

- **New plastic ID-1 licences** (issued from 2011) — a standard card with categories. Generally accepted without problems; only a sworn translation is needed.
- **Old-format licences** (paper or early plastic) carry a higher risk that the urząd will ask for additional confirmation — an apostille or a notarised attestation of authenticity. If your document is this type, it is sensible to obtain the apostille in advance.

If the licence shows a mismatch with your passport (e.g. a changed surname), that is a separate ground for additional checks — attach a marriage or name-change certificate with a sworn translation.

## Where to exchange in Warsaw

In Warsaw, wymiana zagranicznego prawa jazdy (exchange of a foreign licence) takes place at the Wydział Komunikacji of the Urząd Dzielnicy that covers your meldunek:

- Śródmieście — ul. Nowogrodzka 43
- Mokotów — ul. Rakowiecka 25/27
- Ursynów — al. Komisji Edukacji Narodowej 61
- Wola — al. Solidarności 90
- Praga-Południe — ul. Grochowska 274
- Bemowo — ul. Powstańców Śląskich 70

Booking — via the warszawa19115.pl portal or the Warszawa 19115 app. Section: "Wydział Komunikacji" → "Wymiana zagranicznego prawa jazdy". In the busy season, book 3 weeks ahead.

## Documents — the full list

**Mandatory for everyone:**

- Wniosek o wydanie prawa jazdy — the completed application form in Polish (issued at the counter or downloadable from warszawa19115.pl)
- Russian licence — original + a copy of both sides (a good photo, no glare or shadows)
- Tłumaczenie przysięgłe of the licence into Polish from a tłumacz przysięgły of the Russian language (70-110 PLN, 1-3 business days)
- Passport — original + a copy of the photo page
- Proof of legalny pobyt (legal residence): karta pobytu, a D visa, or meldunek + grounds
- Photo 35 × 45 mm — front-facing, no headwear, not older than 6 months
- Payment: 100.00 PLN opłata komunikacyjna + 0.50 PLN opłata ewidencyjna

**Additionally — where a request is likely (old format, data mismatch):**

- Apostille on the licence or a notarised confirmation of authenticity — obtained in advance, about 60 PLN + time

**For categories C, D, C+E, D+E:**

- Valid badania lekarskie (180-250 PLN)
- Valid badania psychologiczne (150 PLN)
- A Kod 95 certificate for professional driving

## Step by step

**Step 1. Translation (and apostille, if needed).** You go to a tłumacz przysięgły of Russian. If you have an old-format licence, obtain the apostille or notarised confirmation of authenticity in advance — not at the last moment before the visit.

**Step 2. Booking.** Via warszawa19115.pl, choose the Wydział Komunikacji of your dzielnica → "Wymiana zagranicznego prawa jazdy" → a free slot. The visit takes about 30 minutes.

**Step 3. The urząd visit.** Bring all documents. The urzędnik (official) accepts the wniosek, takes the original licence, issues a zaświadczenie (certificate) of acceptance, creates a PKK (Profil Kandydata na Kierowcę — an electronic case file), and sends the issuance-confirmation request via the consular route.

**Step 4. Waiting.** The request runs longer than usual — realistically 90-120+ days, sometimes longer. You can track the status on info-car.pl by PESEL and surname. If the urząd asks for an apostille or additional confirmation, you provide it.

**Step 5. Collection.** You collect the Polish licence in person, free of charge. The original Russian licence is not returned to you — it is sent to the issuing country. The Polish licence is valid for 30 years for cat. A, B (5 years for C, D).

## Can you drive while waiting

**Legally — no.** From the moment of złożenie wniosku (submitting the application) until you collect the Polish licence, your Russian licence is formally held by the urząd — you have only a zaświadczenie of submission, which is not a driving document. Driving in this period is an offence: a fine of 200 to 2,000 PLN and a temporary driving restriction.

Because the wait for Russian licences is longer — realistically 3-4 months — you need to plan a car-free life in advance. Options for this period:

1. **Polish car-sharing** (Traficar, Vozilla) — you only need a PESEL + a Polish bank card
2. **Public transport** — a ZTM Warszawa season pass
3. Filing via **pełnomocnictwo** does not lift the driving ban, but it does spare you working days spent on urząd visits

## Expired or lost licences

**Expired Russian licence.** Poland will not exchange a licence past its validity date. First renew it in the issuing country, then apply for the exchange. For an expired licence, badania lekarskie may additionally be required.

**Lost original.** Exchange is only possible on the basis of the original — a copy or duplicate without the original is not accepted. First restore the document in the issuing country, then begin the exchange. The alternative is to sit the WORD exams as a "new candidate": faster, but more expensive and not crediting your experience, opening only the category you pass.

## When it is worth engaging a lawyer

Doing it yourself is realistic if you have a new plastic ID-1 licence, no meldunek issues, and time for the translator and the urząd visit. But for Russian licences specifically — because of the longer, less predictable verification — a lawyer's help is justified more often than for neighbouring countries. A lawyer makes sense if:

- You have an old-format licence and there is a risk of an apostille / notarised-confirmation request
- The name on the licence does not match the passport (maiden name, changed name)
- There was a past refusal by the urząd, or the urząd has requested additional proof
- You want to run the case by pełnomocnictwo — without personal urząd visits
- You are arranging a karta pobytu or Kod 95 in parallel — both processes can be combined
