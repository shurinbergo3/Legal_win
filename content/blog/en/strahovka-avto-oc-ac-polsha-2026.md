# Car Insurance in Poland (OC and AC) in 2026: What to Pick and How Not to Overpay

You bought a car in Poland, or drove your own one in - and the first question after registration is always insurance. In Poland you simply cannot get behind the wheel without OC, and for a newly arrived foreigner the quote often comes back two or three times higher than what a Polish neighbour pays for the same car. It is not that insurers "charge extra to foreigners" - it is how the policy is calculated here. Let us go through the difference between OC and AC, what shapes the price in 2026, and how to arrange insurance in Warsaw without paying more than you need to.

---

## OC and AC - what is the difference

OC (ubezpieczenie odpowiedzialności cywilnej, compulsory third-party liability insurance) covers the damage you cause to others, and by law every car in Poland must have it. AC (autocasco, voluntary comprehensive cover) covers damage to your own car - theft, an accident that was your fault, hail, vandalism - and you take it out by choice. Driving without OC is banned and carries heavy fines from the UFG; driving without AC is fine.

Put simply: OC protects the other person's car and shields your wallet from their claims, while AC protects the car you own. For an old, cheap vehicle many drivers take OC only; for a new or financed car, AC is close to essential.

### What else comes in a package

- **NNW** - personal accident cover for the driver and passengers;
- **Assistance** - roadside help, towing, a replacement car;
- **ochrona prawna** - legal protection;
- **szyby** - separate glass cover.

These add-ons are optional. They get bundled into packages, but you do not actually need all of them - and this is exactly where to look closely when you compare offers.

## What OC costs in 2026

Compulsory OC in 2026 runs on average around 650-900 PLN a year for an experienced driver with a clean record. But that is a hospital-average figure: for a young driver, someone with no Polish history, or a powerful car, the price easily doubles or triples. AC comes on top and depends on the value and model of the car.

The final number is set not by a price list but by your profile. Here is what feeds into it.

---

## What the price is built from

Polish insurers calculate a policy from a set of factors, and almost all of them are tied to data in a central database.

| Factor | How it affects the price |
|--------|--------------------------|
| Claims history (historia szkodowa) | The main factor; claims push it up, a clean record brings it down |
| Driving experience and age | Young and inexperienced drivers pay more |
| No-claims discounts (zniżki) | Up to 60-70% after years of a clean record |
| Engine power and make of car | More powerful and more expensive means a pricier policy |
| Place of registration / residence | Big cities like Warsaw are usually dearer |
| Mileage and how the car is used | Affects AC |

The key point: your full claims and policy history lives in the UFG database (Ubezpieczeniowy Fundusz Gwarancyjny, the Insurance Guarantee Fund), which every insurer can access. That is where a company sees whether you have had accidents and whether you qualify for discounts.

---

## Why a foreigner without a PESEL pays more

This is the real reason for the "eye-watering" quotes new arrivals get. Discounts (zniżki) are granted on the basis of your history as an OC holder - sometimes going back up to ten years. But that history is tied to a PESEL and the Polish database. Without a PESEL the system does not "know" you: no history, no discounts, and the policy is priced at the top rate, as if for a novice with no experience at all.

What that means in practice:

- arranging a full policy that reflects your history without a PESEL is close to impossible - this is not a "just show your passport" myth, but a genuine limit built into the system;
- Polish insurers generally do not automatically credit driving experience earned abroad;
- until you have a PESEL and a Polish history, you pay at the top of the scale.

So the first step towards a sensible insurance price is getting a PESEL. Without it, the conversation about discounts does not even start.

## How to arrange insurance in Warsaw without overpaying

Online calculators are convenient, but for a foreigner with no history they almost always spit out an inflated price - they cannot handle a non-standard profile. In practice a live agent or broker finds options a calculator will never show you.

What actually lowers the price:

1. **Get a PESEL** and start building a Polish OC history in your own name.
2. **Compare several insurers** rather than taking the first package the dealer offers. The spread on the very same car can be several times over.
3. **Do not pay for add-ons** in the package that you do not need.
4. **For a car with no history and a high policy price**, a dealer package can sometimes work out cheaper, since it prices from the car's value rather than the driver's profile.
5. **Buy OC on time** - a gap in cover (przerwa w OC) draws a fine from the UFG and damages your history.

## What happens to the insurance when you buy a car

If you buy a car that is already insured, the active OC passes to you along with the vehicle until the term ends - you can drive legally straight away. But the insurer will recalculate the policy for your profile, and you have the right to cancel it and take out your own OC wherever it is cheaper. Do not forget to notify the insurer of the change of owner. For more on the transfer itself, see the separate article on [transferring a car after purchase](/en/blog/pereoformlenie-avto-przerejestrowanie-2026).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is car insurance compulsory in Poland?**
OC is compulsory for every registered car - you may not drive, or even simply keep, an uninsured vehicle, and the UFG (Insurance Guarantee Fund) fines you for it. AC (comprehensive) is voluntary and taken by choice, most often for new and financed cars.

**Can you take out OC without a PESEL?**
Fully, reflecting your history and discounts - realistically no: the system ties your OC history and zniżki to a PESEL and the Polish database. Without a PESEL the policy is priced at the maximum rate. So it is worth getting a PESEL first and arranging insurance afterwards.

**Why was I quoted such an expensive policy?**
Most likely you have no Polish OC history and no no-claims discount - without them the price runs at the top of the scale, regardless of your experience abroad. As you build a clean history in your own name, the price comes down, sometimes very substantially.

**Does the insurance transfer when you buy a car?**
Yes, the active OC passes to the buyer along with the vehicle until the term ends, so you can drive at once. But the insurer will recalculate the policy for your profile, and you are free to cancel it and buy your own OC for less.

**Which to choose - OC only or OC plus AC?**
For an old, inexpensive car, compulsory OC is often enough. For a new, expensive or financed car it makes sense to add AC to cover damage to your own vehicle. It is all individual - it depends on the car's value and your budget.
