# Continuous Five-Year Residence for PR: How Absences Are Counted in 2026

You're sure you've put in your five years in Poland and it's time to apply for karta stałego pobytu (permanent residence card). Then the wojewoda (regional governor), checking your stamps, finds an extra month abroad - and an odmowa (refusal) arrives. Continuous five-year residence is the most treacherous requirement for PR: it looks simple until you start counting the days of trips, work postings and visits home. A counting error resets the whole counter, and the five years start over.

Here's exactly how ciągłość pobytu (continuity of residence) is calculated in 2026: the six-months-a-year and ten-months-across-five-years rule, what the urząd treats as a break, how the wojewoda verifies your history, how to count it yourself, and what to do if you have a doubtful absence.

---

## How the five-year continuity is calculated

Residence counts as continuous if your absences from Poland don't exceed 6 months in total in any single year of the five, and no more than 10 months in total across all five years together. On top of that, your status must never lapse: an expired card, an overstay, or a gap between permits resets the whole counter and forces the five years to begin again.

So two limits apply at once. The first is about physical absence: the days spent abroad are counted. The second is about legal status: at every point in the five years you must hold a valid basis of stay, with not a single gap. You can fail on either one, and a failure on either resets the counter.

Let's take them separately - because they get confused constantly.

---

## The six-months-a-year and ten-across-five rule

This is the limit on physical absence. The formula:

- **No more than 6 months** consecutively or in total in any of the five years.
- **No more than 10 months** in total across the whole five-year period.

Both conditions have to hold together. You can stay inside 6 months every single year, but if the five-year total tips past 10, continuity is broken. And the reverse: even with a modest overall total, one year exceeding 6 months breaks everything.

| Year | Days abroad | Within 6 months? |
|------|-------------|------------------|
| Year 1 | 40 | Yes |
| Year 2 | 150 | Yes (around 5 months) |
| Year 3 | 30 | Yes |
| Year 4 | 60 | Yes |
| Year 5 | 20 | Yes |
| **Total** | **300 (around 10 months)** | On the edge |

In this example the person is right at the line: about 10 months across the five years. One more trip and it's an odmowa. This is exactly what the borderline cases we handle most often look like: people count "by feel" when they need to count by the day.

## What resets the counter

A break comes in two forms - by status and by absence. Both are equally dangerous.

### An expired card or a gap between permits

The most common one. Your karta pobytu (residence card) ran out and you applied late for the next one - a gap of even a few days opened between the old permit and the new. To the wojewoda that's a break in legal residence, and the five years reset. What to do when a card has already expired we cover in the piece on [an expired karta pobytu](/en/blog/karta-pobytu-wygasla-co-delat).

### An overstay

You stayed in the country with no valid basis - even for a day. Nielegalny pobyt (unlawful stay) doesn't just interrupt the counter; it creates a separate problem for future applications.

### Long work postings and extended departures

Jobs with long trips abroad, caring for a relative back home, getting stuck outside Poland - all of it piles up days of absence. A single seven-month posting in one year already breaks the six-month rule, even if you were formally employed by a Polish company the whole time.

### Changing basis with a gap

Moving, say, from a student card to a work card doesn't break continuity in itself - but only if there was no gap in status between them. If there was a gap, the counter resets.

---

## Study counts only half

A separate rule for those who studied. Time spent on student status counts toward the five years only at half value - under Article 65 of the Ustawa o cudzoziemcach (Foreigners Act). So four years of study on a student card give only two years of credit toward stały pobyt.

This upends the plans of many graduates: a person is sure they've lived in Poland five years since enrolling, when in fact half that time "weighs" only half as much. The way to count it: full years after finishing your studies (on a work card, through marriage and so on) run one-to-one, while student years are halved. If your history is mixed - study plus work - the total has to be added up carefully, period by period.

---

## How the wojewoda checks your history

The urząd doesn't take your word for it. Continuity is verified against several sources at once, and any discrepancy between them is a ready-made reason for an odmowa.

- **Passport stamps** - entries and exits at borders where they're still applied.
- **The EES system** - with its launch, exits and entries to the Schengen area are logged electronically, and "forgetting" a trip is no longer possible. How it works we cover in the piece on [the EES system at EU borders](/en/blog/ees-system-vyezd-vyezd-evrosoyuz).
- **Permit history** - all your cards, visas, decyzje (decisions) and application receipts by date.
- **Employment and meldunek data** - ZUS (the social insurance institution), the tax office, registration by address.

The wojewoda assembles this picture and compares it to what you declared in the wniosek (application). If you stated you didn't travel and the EES shows two trips, that's not only a break but a question mark over the truthfulness of your declaration. So you're better off knowing your own history more precisely than the urząd knows it.

## How to count it yourself

Before you apply, run your own residence audit. Step by step:

1. **Gather every document** - passports with stamps, old cards, tickets, application receipts, employment certificates.
2. **Build the status line** - day by day from the start of the five years to today. Make sure a valid basis existed at every point, with no gaps.
3. **Write out every absence** - date of departure, date of return, number of days. Year by year, separately.
4. **Check both limits** - the total per year up to 6 months, the total across the five years up to 10 months.
5. **Halve the student years** - if you have any in your history.
6. **Find the doubtful spots** - borderline totals, short gaps in status, long trips.

If after counting you're clearly clear with room to spare, you can apply. If you come out on the edge or find a gap, stop - strategy first.

---

## What to do with a doubtful break

Not every break is fatal, and not every borderline count is a verdict. The options depend on the situation:

- **A borderline total of absences** - you can wait until the "heavy" year drops out of the five-year window and apply later with a clean calculation.
- **A short gap in status** - it can sometimes be shown that the next card was applied for on time and continuity was formally preserved.
- **A doubtful count by the urząd** - if the wojewoda counted the days wrong, that's challenged through an odwołanie. The mechanics of appealing we cover in the piece on [a permanent residence refusal and the odwołanie](/en/blog/otkaz-po-karte-stalego-pobytu-odwolanie-2026).
- **A genuine large break** - the honest move is to change the basis: apply for stały pobyt through marriage, through Polish descent, or another route where the five-year record isn't needed.

The main thing is not to apply blind. An odmowa over a break loses not only the fee and months of waiting but also leaves a mark on your application history.
