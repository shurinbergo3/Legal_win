# Criminal record certificates for licences: security, transport, finance

In some jobs a criminal record certificate is a matter of employer trust. In others it is written into statute as a condition of entry, and without it the licence is simply not issued. In that second category a foreigner needs not one document but a set: the Polish one plus a national one.

Here are the sectors where we assemble those packs most often.

## Where a certificate is a statutory condition

| Sector | Requirement | Who needs the certificate |
|---|---|---|
| Physical security | entry on the list of qualified security staff | the employee |
| Road transport | carrier good repute | the operator and the transport manager |
| Taxi and passenger transport | passenger transport licence | the operator and drivers |
| Finance, insurance | KNF and sector-specific requirements | board members, agents, brokers |
| Bookkeeping, tax advisory | licensed bookkeeping, tax adviser status | the person carrying out the activity |
| Detective services | detective licence | the detective |
| Firearms | firearms permit | the applicant |
| Healthcare and care work | sector rules plus employer requirement | staff |
| Work with minors | the Kamilek Act, a separate regime | everyone admitted to the activity |

The last row runs its own regime, with the RSTPN register and a check of countries of residence over 20 years - [we cover it separately](/en/blog/krk-i-rstpn-rabota-s-detmi-2026).

## Security: qualified security staff

Entry on the list requires confirmed qualifications, medical and psychological opinions, and **no conviction for an intentional offence or an intentional fiscal offence**.

For a foreigner, three things are added: a residence title carrying the right to work, Polish sufficient for the role, and a certificate from the country of citizenship.

A practical note: request the full KRK certificate rather than a narrow scope. The police commander maintaining the list reviews all entries.

## Transport: carrier good repute

The requirement is doubled. Good repute is verified for the **operator** and for the **transport manager** holding a Certificate of Professional Competence.

What goes to the road transport inspectorate or the district office with the carrier authorisation application:

- declarations of no criminal record from each board member;
- a KRK certificate where the authority requests one;
- for foreigners, a certificate from their country of citizenship with a sworn translation.

Good repute is lost not only by a conviction but by a run of transport offences - [we wrote about ITD penalties here](/en/blog/shtrafy-itd-bag-12000-pln-vodiel).

## Finance, bookkeeping, insurance

The list of requirements is broader than expected:

- **board members** of financial institutions undergo suitability assessment;
- **insurance agents** must show no conviction for offences against property, documents or economic transactions;
- **tax advisers** and providers of bookkeeping services face their own professional requirements;
- **beneficial owners and board members** face bank AML checks generally.

For the company itself, a collective entity certificate is often needed too - [covered separately](/en/blog/krk-o-podmiocie-zbiorowym-dlya-firmy-2026).

## What a foreigner adds

The pattern is identical across all the sectors above:

1. **The Polish KRK certificate** - PLN 20-30, online or at an information point. Without a PESEL, [a second channel applies](/en/blog/krk-dlya-inostranca-bez-pesel-2026).
2. **A certificate from your country of citizenship** - the Ukrainian interior ministry certificate, the Turkish adli sicil kaydı, the Belarusian, Georgian or Kazakh equivalent.
3. **Legalisation** - bilateral treaties exempt Ukraine, Belarus and Russia from apostille; most other countries need one. [How to identify your case](/en/blog/apostille-ili-legalizacja-konsularna-2026).
4. **A sworn translation** into Polish, always.

Timings: the Polish certificate from one day, the foreign one from two weeks to three months. The second is usually what sets your start date.

## How fresh it must be

There is no single rule; the benchmarks are:

- licensing authorities - no older than **three months**;
- tenders and public procurement - up to **six months**;
- employers in regulated sectors - three months on the date of admission.

Ordering far in advance makes no sense: at PLN 20 it is simpler to pull a fresh one for the specific filing.

## Frequently asked questions

**My conviction was spent. What will the certificate show?**
Once a conviction is expunged the entry is removed and the certificate comes back clean. The periods depend on the sentence and are calculated case by case.

**The conviction was in my home country, not Poland. Is it visible?**
Not in the Polish register - which is exactly why licensing bodies require the national certificate. Concealing it makes no sense: discrepancies surface, and the consequences outweigh the conviction.

**Can I work in security with a conviction for a negligent offence?**
The statute speaks of intentional offences. Each case is assessed individually by the police commander; the legal classification decides, not the existence of an entry.

**Who pays for the certificates in regulated sectors?**
Usually the candidate, though some employers in transport and security reimburse it.

**Is a certificate needed when renewing a licence?**
As a rule, yes - renewals typically involve a full re-check of the conditions.

**My home country takes three months to issue one.**
Start before signing the contract. In several jurisdictions we can speed things up through a consulate or a local representative.
