# ETIAS from late 2026: who needs it and who is exempt

Europe is finishing its rebuild of border control. Two systems get confused constantly:

- **EES** (Entry/Exit System) - electronic registration of entries and exits with biometrics. It launched on 12 October 2025 and has been fully operational at every external border crossing point **since 10 April 2026**.
- **ETIAS** - a pre-travel authorisation for nationals of visa-exempt countries. Launch is expected in **Q4 2026**, with the exact date announced in advance.

The question our clients actually ask is whether it affects them. Usually not - provided you hold a residence card.

## Who is exempt from ETIAS

ETIAS is designed for nationals of roughly 59 visa-exempt countries travelling to the Schengen area **for short stays** of up to 90 days in any 180.

Exempt:

- **holders of a Karta Pobytu** and other Schengen residence permits;
- holders of **national D visas**;
- EU, EEA and Swiss nationals;
- **family members of EU nationals** exercising free movement rights with the relevant document;
- holders of long-stay visas from other Schengen states.

The logic is simple: ETIAS serves visa-free travel. A stay already legalised by a permit is not visa-free travel, so ETIAS does not apply.

EES follows the same logic - it registers short-stay travellers, and residence permit holders fall outside its scope.

## Who will need ETIAS

| Citizenship | Entry regime | ETIAS |
|---|---|---|
| Ukraine (biometric passport) | visa-free | yes, once live |
| Georgia, Moldova, Albania, Serbia | visa-free | yes |
| UK, US, Canada, Japan | visa-free | yes |
| Turkey | Schengen visa | not applicable |
| Russia, Belarus, Kazakhstan | Schengen visa | not applicable |
| Holder of a Schengen residence card | residence permit | no |

One distinction matters: **ETIAS does not replace a visa**. Nationals who need a Schengen visa neither apply for nor require ETIAS - their procedure is different.

## How it will work

The application is filed online before travel. Expected parameters:

- a **EUR 20 fee** for applicants aged 18 to 70; free for those under 18 and over 70;
- **validity of three years**, or until the passport expires, whichever comes first;
- **multiple entries** within the 90/180 rule;
- a decision usually within minutes, or days where additional checks apply.

ETIAS attaches to a specific passport. Replace the passport and you apply again, even if the authorisation has not expired - the same logic as the American ESTA.

## What it means if you live in Poland

Three practical consequences.

**1. The residence card gets more useful.** Beyond the right to live and work, it takes you outside both ETIAS and EES - noticeable time saved at borders if you travel often.

**2. The 90/180 rule has not gone anywhere.** EES now counts your days automatically, without stamps and without room for error. A border officer used to count stamps by hand and might miss an overstay; the system now shows the exact balance. For anyone living between countries that is a material change - [how 90/180 is counted](/en/blog/ees-system-vyezd-vyezd-evrosoyuz).

**3. Moving from visa-free travel to a residence card makes even more sense**, particularly for Ukrainian nationals as UKR status winds down - [deadlines and the transition](/en/blog/pesel-ukr-aktualizacja-danych-31-avgusta-2026).

## Common misunderstandings

**"ETIAS is a visa."** No. It is a pre-travel authorisation for visa-free entry, comparable to the American ESTA or the British ETA.

**"ETIAS grants the right to work."** No. Work rights come from a work permit.

**"With ETIAS I can live in Europe for three years."** No. Three years is the authorisation's validity; the stay stays capped at 90 days in 180.

**"With ETIAS they must let me in."** No. The border officer always makes the final call.

## Frequently asked questions

**When exactly does ETIAS launch?**
Q4 2026 is expected. The EU announces the date months in advance, and a transitional period follows the launch.

**Does a child need ETIAS?**
Yes, every traveller needs an authorisation, though it is free for those under 18 and over 70.

**What happens if I travel without ETIAS after launch?**
The carrier must check the authorisation at boarding, so in practice you will not be allowed on the flight.

**I hold a residence card but a third-country passport. Do I need ETIAS?**
No. A valid Schengen residence permit exempts you. Carry the card with your passport.

**I filed for a residence card and hold a UPO. Does that exempt me?**
The UPO confirms filing and legalises your stay in Poland, but it is not a residence permit for ETIAS purposes. Travel outside Poland while the case is pending needs separate analysis.

**Does ETIAS replace fingerprints at the border?**
No, they are different systems. EES collects biometrics at the border; ETIAS is a pre-travel check.
