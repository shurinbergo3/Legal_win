# Meldunek in Poland 2026: address registration for foreigners

Meldunek (address registration) is the most underrated formality in Poland. People postpone it for months, until something stalls: the bank will not open a business account, the school will not enrol the child, the wydział komunikacji (vehicle registration department) refuses to register a car, and the wojewoda (regional governor) asks for proof of where you actually live.

The short version: a national of a country outside the EU must register **by the fourth day** after moving into an address, provided the stay there exceeds 30 days. Registration is free and takes one visit to the urząd (municipal office). From 1 January 2026, however, filing online requires an active eDoręczenia (electronic delivery) account - and non-EU foreigners cannot file online at all.

## Who must register and how quickly

The obligation comes from the ustawa o ewidencji ludności (Population Records Act). The deadlines differ, and mixing them up is expensive:

| Who | Deadline | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| National of a non-EU country | 4th day after moving in | stay longer than 30 days |
| EU, EEA or Swiss national | 30 days after arrival | stay longer than 30 days |
| Family member of an EU national | 30 days | stay longer than 30 days |
| Polish citizen | 30 days | change of residence |

Four days is not a typo. That deadline applies to Ukrainians, Belarusians, Russians, Georgians, Turks, Kazakhs and every other third-country national. The clock starts on the day you move into a specific address, not on the day you crossed the border.

Missing the deadline is a wykroczenie (petty offence) under art. 147 of the Kodeks wykroczeń (Petty Offences Code). Polish citizens are effectively not sanctioned for it; foreigners can be - a mandat (on-the-spot fine) or a court case with a grzywna (fine) of up to PLN 5,000. Municipal offices rarely impose it, but the Straż Graniczna (Border Guard) does check registration when verifying the legality of your stay.

## Two kinds of registration

**Pobyt czasowy (temporary registration)** is the standard option for a foreigner. It is granted for the period you declare, but never beyond the validity of your residence document: a national D visa, a Karta Pobytu (residence card) or the visa-free window. After you collect a new card, you file the notification again.

**Pobyt stały (permanent registration)** is available only to holders of a Karta Stałego Pobytu (permanent residence card), EU long-term resident status, and Polish citizens. It has no expiry date.

The third item people ask about most is the **adres do korespondencji** (correspondence address). That is not registration. You give it to the tax office, to the KRS (company register) and in company documents, but it never reaches the PESEL register and does not substitute for registration where registration is what the law asks for. We compare the two in a [separate article on temporary and permanent registration](/en/blog/meldunek-czasowy-vs-staly-2026).

## What you need to bring

The pack is short, and in nine cases out of ten the obstacle is not paperwork but the property owner:

- passport (original);
- proof of legal stay: national D visa, residence card, visa-free entry stamp or the UPO receipt from a filed application;
- the owner's title to the flat: akt własności (deed), an extract from the księga wieczysta (land and mortgage register) or a umowa najmu (lease agreement);
- the właściciel's (owner's) signature on the notification form, or their presence at the office;
- a completed zgłoszenie pobytu czasowego cudzoziemca form;
- for a child - a birth certificate with a sworn translation.

The critical detail is the form of consent: the owner signs **on the form itself**, in front of the clerk or in advance with a notarised signature. A lease agreement alone, without the owner's signature, will not be accepted. If your landlord refuses, the case is not lost - it runs through a separate administrative procedure, [which we cover here](/en/blog/zameldowanie-bez-soglasiya-sobstvennika-2026).

## Online or in person: what changed on 1 January 2026

Since 1 January 2026, electronic filing through mObywatel and gov.pl requires an **active eDoręczenia address**. A Profil Zaufany (trusted profile) alone is no longer enough - the same shift that reshaped correspondence with the wojewoda in residence cases.

And the detail that saves you an evening: **non-EU foreigners cannot register online**. The electronic form remains available to Polish and EU citizens. Everyone else attends the urząd gminy or urząd dzielnicy in person with original documents, or sends a representative holding a power of attorney.

The appointment itself takes 10-20 minutes: submit the form, have the documents checked, and the entry lands in the PESEL register the same day. Fee: PLN 0.

## What it costs

Registration itself is always free. You only pay for certificates:

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Registration (temporary or permanent) | PLN 0 |
| Certificate of permanent registration | PLN 0 |
| Certificate of temporary registration | PLN 17 |
| Power of attorney (stamp duty) | PLN 17 |

The zaświadczenie (certificate) is what banks want when opening a business account, and what the tax office, the insurer, the school and the vehicle department ask for. We mapped out which document goes where in a [separate breakdown](/en/blog/zaswiadczenie-o-zameldowaniu-2026).

## Why registration matters in practice

Missing registration rarely hurts immediately. It hurts the moment something becomes urgent:

1. **Residence card.** The wojewoda expects proof of where you live. A lease can work, but registration removes the question entirely.
2. **PESEL.** Registering for a stay longer than 30 days triggers a PESEL number automatically, with no separate application. It is the fastest route to a Profil Zaufany and mDowód.
3. **School and nursery.** Compulsory schooling is tied to place of residence; without registration a child is admitted only if places remain.
4. **Vehicle registration.** The vehicle department pulls the owner's address from the register.
5. **Business account and AML checks.** Banks ask for address confirmation - one certificate closes the requirement.
6. **NFZ and choosing a clinic.** Your primary care facility is assigned by address.
7. **Local elections.** EU nationals with registration may vote in municipal elections.

## What registration does not do

Three myths that make owners refuse to sign:

- **It grants no rights to the flat.** Registration is a records entry, not a legal title. It does not block a sale and creates no "share".
- **A registered person can be deregistered.** Once a tenant moves out, the owner files for administrative deregistration. The procedure takes weeks, but it works.
- **It does not legalise your stay.** It replaces neither a visa nor a residence card, and grants no right to work.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I register without a lease agreement?**
Yes, if the owner signs the form and shows their title to the property. A lease is convenient evidence, but the statute requires legal title from the owner, not from you.

**What if I live in a dormitory or employer accommodation?**
The building administrator or the employer as property manager signs the form. Many companies do this centrally - ask HR first.

**Is registration linked to my residence card?**
They are two independent procedures, but temporary registration cannot outlast your residence document. Once you collect a new card, file the notification again.

**I rent informally and the owner refuses. Any way out?**
Several: administrative proceedings to establish the fact of residence, a change of flat, or a lease that expressly grants the right to register. We break it down [here](/en/blog/zameldowanie-bez-soglasiya-sobstvennika-2026).

**Do offices actually fine people for not registering?**
Rarely. But during a Border Guard check, a residence card application or an employer inspection, an address missing from the register raises questions you will answer in writing.
