# Opening a Bank Account in Poland for Foreigners - 2026

The Polish banking system is one of the most accessible to foreigners in the EU. With a PESEL, an account opens in 30-60 minutes; without one, it takes 1-2 days (some banks require a visa or karta pobytu). In 2026, competition between banks has produced essentially free basic accounts, mobile apps with translations into Russian, Ukrainian, and English, and a streamlined process for newcomers. Below - a comparison of the main players, documents required, and pitfalls to know.

Most LegalWin clients start with **PKO BP** or **Santander** - they accept almost everyone, including those on a D visa without PESEL.

## Documents for Account Opening

**With PESEL and karta pobytu:**

- Passport (original + copy)
- Karta pobytu or its issuance decision
- Address confirmation in Poland (meldunek or umowa najmu)
- Polish phone number
- Email (any reliable domain - Gmail, Outlook, ProtonMail)

**With D visa, no PESEL:**

- Passport (original + copy of visa page)
- D visa or its issuance confirmation
- Address confirmation in Poland (umowa najmu or employer's confirmation)
- Polish phone number

**On visa-free regime (UA, MD, GE):**

- Passport
- Accommodation proof (hotel, AirBnB, lease)
- Confirmation of visit purpose (employment contract, employer's oświadczenie, document from educational institution)
- Polish phone number

In 2026, some banks (especially PKO BP and Santander) may open a basic account on visa-free regime without PESEL - but with limits on withdrawals and transactions until karta pobytu is received.

## Step by Step

**Step 1.** Get a Polish SIM card (Play, Plus, Orange, T-Mobile - 5-15 PLN starter). No bank opens an account without a Polish number.

**Step 2.** Decide whether in-person presence is required or online suffices.

- **In-person** - mandatory for non-residents without PESEL, for everyone at Pekao, for USD/EUR accounts
- **Online** - possible at PKO BP IKO, mBank, ING, Millennium with active PESEL and Profil Zaufany

**Step 3.** Choose a branch or download the app. In Warsaw, each of the 5 main banks has 50-100 branches.

**Step 4.** Booking or walk-in. Branches accept by appointment (via the bank's website or app) or walk-in - but waiting time is 30-60 minutes without an appointment.

**Step 5.** Document signing. With PESEL - 30-45 minutes. Without PESEL - 1-2 hours due to additional KRS/CEIDG verification.

**Step 6.** Receiving the card. The debit card arrives at your address in 7-14 days (or a "temporary" card is issued at the branch on the day of account opening).

## Fee Comparison in 2026

All 5 major banks in 2026 offer **a free basic account** if conditions are met:

- At least 1 transaction per month (card payments or transfers)
- At least 1 use of online banking per month
- No automatic monthly account fees (with the basic pakiet)

Additional options:

| Option | Cost in 2026 |
|---|---|
| Visa/MasterCard debit card | free (with active account) |
| Withdrawals at own-bank ATM | free |
| Withdrawals at other banks' ATMs | 5-10 PLN or free via Euronet (PKO, Pekao) |
| Internal bank transfer | free |
| Transfer to other Polish banks | free (Express Elixir or instant) |
| SWIFT international transfer | 0-30 PLN + correspondent bank commission |
| EUR/USD card | free or 5-10 PLN/month |

For a **business account** (sp. z o.o. or JDG) - 30-50 PLN/month for obsługa firmowa, with separate apps (mBank Firmy, Santander Firma).

## Pitfalls - What to Know

**1. AML checks on large transfers.** Receiving a transfer over 15,000 PLN without a clear history - the bank may temporarily freeze and request documents (sender, source, basis). Prepare documents in advance.

**2. Distinction between "konto rezydenta" and "konto nierezydenta".** Until PESEL is received, you typically have "konto nierezydenta" with limits. Afterwards - switched to resident on request, free of charge.

**3. Sanctions on RU citizens.** In 2026 most Polish banks do not open accounts for RU citizens without an active karta pobytu. With karta pobytu - they do, but with additional checks.

**4. Address verification checks.** Banks often require someone to sign for your address (e.g., entrance service or postal courier). This is needed for verification.

**5. Business account and VAT.** If you have an sp.z.o.o. or JDG, a business account is mandatory for VAT settlements. Without one, ZUS accepts payments, but NIP-related payments do not work.

## Specifics for Different Groups

**Students:** all banks offer a "student account" with extra bonuses (free cards, free international transfers, cashback on purchases). Required: passport, D visa or student karta pobytu, university enrolment confirmation.

**Corporate employees:** if relocating with a major employer (Deloitte, Accenture, Microsoft Polska), HR usually issues a recommendation letter to the bank, easing the process.

**Freelancers with foreign clients:** Revolut Polska + a basic Polish account is the optimal combination. Revolut for USD/EUR transfers, the Polish account for ZUS, PIT, and rent.
